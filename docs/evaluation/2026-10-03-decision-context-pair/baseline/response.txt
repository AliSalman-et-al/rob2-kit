## Real case: AWARD-CHN3

### 2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?

**Permitted response: probably_no.**

The report says all 291 randomized participants were included in the ITT population, and Figure 2’s caption describes the HbA1c analyses as ITT (article p. 4, lines 1–2; p. 5, lines 6–7). But for efficacy, data were censored from the point of rescue therapy or premature study-drug discontinuation (p. 3, lines 89–93). That makes the stated analysis population insufficient to establish that all randomized participants’ available Week 28 outcomes contributed to the assignment comparison.

For this result, the report does not say how many participants had measured Week 28 HbA1c values omitted after those events, whether censoring applied to this endpoint in a way that excluded observed values, or what the primary MMRM did with missing measurements. The primary result uses MMRM (p. 3, lines 94–100; p. 5, lines 2–7), and the supplementary LOCF analysis concerns secondary composite endpoints, not this result (supplement PDF p. 2, lines 1–2, 43–47). Registry results describe a population with baseline and at least one post-baseline HbA1c value and report 133 and 142 participants (registry p. 1, lines 1904–1918), but that alone does not establish whether eligible participants with observed Week 28 values were excluded or analysed outside their randomized groups.

**Material unknowns and alternative explanation:** The report’s explicit ITT label and MMRM method support a possible appropriate assignment analysis. Rescue therapy was used by only one participant, in the dulaglutide group (article p. 7, lines 7–9), which limits concern about rescue-related exclusions. However, the report does not provide endpoint-specific analysis counts or clarify the consequences of censoring after discontinuation. Study completion counts do not resolve this: completion is not the same as Week 28 HbA1c availability (registry p. 1, lines 2252–2261; visual Figure 1 evidence, p. 4, lines 1–5).

### 2.7 If N/PN/NI to 2.6: Was there potential for a substantial impact on the result?

**Permitted response: probably_no.**

This is activated by the 2.6 judgment. The only explicitly identified rescue-therapy case is one participant in the dulaglutide group (article p. 7, lines 7–9). The report also describes 15 participants as not completing the study, with 10 in the dulaglutide group and 5 in the placebo group (registry p. 1, lines 2252–2261). Those counts do not establish how many Week 28 HbA1c measurements were unavailable or omitted from analysis, or that all noncompleters were excluded.

The available evidence therefore supports a probable judgment of limited potential impact, given the single rescue case and relatively few study noncompletions. **Unknown:** the endpoint-specific missingness, analysis exclusions, and prognostic characteristics of affected participants are not reported here. The imbalanced noncompletion counts leave some possibility of impact, so the conclusion is not certain.

## Fictional controls: question 2.6 only

**Control A — probably_yes.** Of 100 eligible randomized participants, the 10 excluded had unmeasured endpoints, while all 90 measured outcomes were analysed in their assigned groups; other outcomes were retained regardless of treatment stopping (synthetic control, p. 1, lines 1–3). This is an appropriate modified ITT analysis. The stipulated facts leave no material uncertainty about the exclusion mechanism.

**Control B — no.** All 100 endpoint values were measured, but the analysis omitted the observed values of 10 noncompleters based on treatment completion, despite retaining their randomized groups (synthetic control, p. 1, lines 2–3). This is a post-randomization, adherence-based exclusion from the assignment analysis. No causal adjustment is reported.
