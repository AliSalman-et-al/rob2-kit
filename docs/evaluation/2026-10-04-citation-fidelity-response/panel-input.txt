19|4.1.2.2
20|Assumption on Non-informative Censoring
21|The time to event analysis (Cox regression) relies on the assumption of non-informative
22|censoring. To examine this assumption, variables that may be related to censoring, for
23|example, the most frequent major protocol deviation, certain SAEs, will be explored. Rates
24|per 100 patient-years will be calculated per treatment group and compared between patients
25|censored early without complete follow-up, and those with complete follow-up.
26|To assess possible effects of informative censoring on the primary efficacy endpoint,
27|sensitivity analyses will be done as follows. First, the tipping point analysis will be performed
28|where in patients who prematurely discontinued the study without having a primary endpoint
29|event prior to discontinuation, events will be imputed during their missing follow up time (i.e.
30|time from censoring to trial termination visit) under various scenarios for the hazard rates for
31|non-completers in each arm. For each scenario, 2000 imputations will be performed and the
32|results will be combined across 2000 combined datasets (actual events in completers +
33|imputed events in non-completers) using SAS PROC MIANALYZE. Specifically, log-hazard
34|ratio estimate of treatment effect will be calculated as an average and its associated variance
35|will be obtained using Rubin's8 (1987) formula, which combines within-imputation and
36|between-imputation variances. Hazard ratios, Wald's 95% CIs and Wald's test p-values will
37|also be calculated. The goal of this analysis is to find scenarios where the primary analysis
38|results will be "tipped", i.e. the conclusion will change.
