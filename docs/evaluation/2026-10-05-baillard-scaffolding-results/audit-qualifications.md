# Source-grounded qualifications after independent review

These qualifications amend the operator's audit, not either frozen model answer.
They are not blinded human gold or a paid reassessment.

- Figure 4 reports participant minima for 26 control and 27 NIV participants
  (PDF p.3, lines 129–131 and inspected figure pixels). Together with the 57
  randomized participants in Figure 2, this supports at least 53 observed minima.
  The other four could have been measured but omitted, or unavailable: zero to
  four missing minima remain possible. Incomplete records do not establish four
  unavailable selected outcomes.
- Conditional materiality illustration: **if** all four selected outcomes were
  unavailable, two per arm, and each possible SpO2 is between 0 and 100%, using
  the rounded reported means 93% (27 NIV) and 81% (26 control) yields a full
  randomized-population mean difference between about 4.23 and 18.27 percentage
  points. The lower end is `(27*93)/29 - (26*81 + 2*100)/28`; the upper end is
  `(27*93 + 2*100)/29 - (26*81)/28`. Under those assumptions direction cannot
  reverse, but magnitude can change from the reported 12-point difference. These
  are illustrative bounds on unknown values using rounded means, not proof of
  missingness, an exact source estimate, automatic Low, a required sensitivity
  analysis or a universal rule for nearly-all availability.
- Methods names a mean drop as primary (PDF p.3, line 113), then immediately uses
  prior during-ETI saturation and a 5% difference in mean saturation during ETI
  in sample-size planning (lines 114–118). The latter positively supports the
  possibility of imprecise naming. Preserve both passages; they establish neither
  a results-driven switch nor verified pre-unblinding finalization.
- Minimal's 5.2 Probably No can be defensible if minimum during ETI was genuinely
  an independently chosen review eligibility criterion. The error would be
  silently treating the reported exact Result as the independent eligible set.
  Other study-stage measurements are not automatically eligible alternatives.
  The audit does not establish a universal wrong 5.2 answer.
- D2 appropriate-analysis Yes/Probably Yes and D3 concerns are compatible when
  exclusions really are missing-outcome-only. The issue here is the unsupported
  or insufficiently qualified exclusion premise. Rich's D2 Some concerns comes
  from 2.3 No information versus Minimal's Probably No, not from the Y/PY
  appropriate-analysis difference. D3 High versus Some concerns comes solely
  from 3.4 No information versus Probably No. Emergency-intubation context can
  support a **possible** outcome-related mechanism at 3.3 without a documented
  actual dependence; it does not establish likelihood at 3.4.
