## Cochrane significance and plausibility

Current official [22August2019RoB2 guidance](https://www.riskofbias.info/welcome/rob-2-0-tool/current-version-of-rob-2), Box8 printedp45: D3.1 concerns whether missing values could importantly change the estimate, not a fixed retention threshold. D3.2 reassurance requires little change across **plausible** missingness assumptions. D3.3 treats withdrawal censoring as missing outcome information even when partial followup contributes to analysis. D3.4 explicitly lists different missingness/censoring proportions between groups as evidence relevant to likely outcome-dependent missingness; reasons and trial circumstances also matter. [Handbook8.5](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-08) distinguishes possible mechanism, likely mechanism and the impact on the selected estimate.

Interpretation: preserved noninferiority does not demonstrate HR stability; HR/CI materially change in this adversarial scenario. This establishes sensitivity, not that100%oflost1-monthpatients and0%oflostcontrols are probable. The cited sources do not demonstrate that scenario's plausibility or a realized bias of that magnitude. Nor does extreme-scenario sensitivity alone force High. Differential loss supplies independent Cochrane-relevant concern; absent reasons do not automatically force NI. A defensible probable assessment may integrate those circumstances, but should not label the extreme outcomes plausible without a supporting argument. Consent-withdrawal impact remains unresolved.
