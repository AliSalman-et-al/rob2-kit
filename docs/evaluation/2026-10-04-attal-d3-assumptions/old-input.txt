46|Summary
47|Background Data from previous studies suggest that botulinum toxin A has analgesic eff ects against peripheral
48|neuropathic pain, but the quality of the evidence is low. We aimed to assess the safety and effi cacy of repeated
49|administrations of botulinum toxin A in patients with neuropathic pain.
50|Methods We did a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial at two outpatient clinics in France (Clinical Pain
51|Centre, Ambroise Paré Hospital, APHP, Boulogne-Billancourt, and Neurological Centre, Hôpital Dupuytren,
52|Limoges) and one in Brazil (Neurological Department, Hospital das Clínicas da FMUSP, São Paulo). Patients aged
53|18-85 years with peripheral neuropathic pain were randomly assigned (1:1) by block randomisation, according to a
54|centralised schedule, to receive two subcutaneous administrations of botulinum toxin A (up to 300 units) or placebo,
55|12 weeks apart. All patients and investigators were masked to treatment assignment. The primary outcome was the
56|effi cacy of botulinum toxin A versus placebo, measured as the change from baseline in self-reported mean weekly
57|pain intensity over the course of 24 weeks from the fi rst administration. The primary effi cacy analysis was a mixed-
58|model repeated-measures analysis in the intention-to-treat population. This trial is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov,
59|NCT01251211.
60|Findings Between Oct 2, 2010, and Aug 2, 2013, 152 patients were enrolled, of whom 68 were randomly assigned
61|(34 per group), and 66 (37 [56%] men) were included in the primary analysis (34 in the botulinum toxin A group and
62|32 in the placebo group). Botulinum toxin A reduced pain intensity over 24 weeks compared with placebo (adjusted
63|eff ect estimate -0·77, 95% CI -0·95 to -0·59; p<0·0001). Pain on injection was the only adverse eff ect reported, and
64|occurred in 19 (56%) participants in the botulinum toxin A group and 17 (53%) of those in the placebo group (p=1·0).
65|Severe pain was experienced by ten (29%) participants in the botulinum toxin A group and 11 (34%) in the placebo
66|group (p=0·8).
67|Interpretation Two administrations of botulinum toxin A, each of which comprised several injections, have a sustained
68|analgesic eff ect against peripheral neuropathic pain. Several factors, such as the presence of allodynia and a limited
69|thermal defi cit, may be useful in predicting treatment response and should be investigated further.
70|Funding Institut National de la Santé et de la Recherche Médicale (INSERM) and Fondation CNP (France).
71|Introduction
72|Botulinum toxin type A is widely used to treat muscle
73|hyperactivity,1 on the basis of its ability to inhibit synaptic
74|exocytosis and, therefore, to disable neural transmission.
75|In the past decade, fi ndings from several experimental
76|studies in animals and healthy people have suggested
77|that botulinum toxin A might have analgesic activity
78|independent of its eff ect on muscle tone,2,3 which has led
79|to the suggestion of new indications for the drug in
80|analgesia, particularly for chronic migraine.4 Findings
81|from prospective studies on dystonia,5 bladder dys-
82|function,6 and chronic migraine4 have shown repeated
83|administrations of botulinum toxin A to be safe, but the
84|safety and effi cacy of repeated administrations of this
85|drug for neuropathic pain have not yet been investigated.
86|Neuropathic pain is a debilitating chronic pain disorder
87|and an unmet medical need.7 Most drugs used for the
88|management of neuropathic pain have dose-limiting
89|adverse eff ects,8 and eff ective drugs without systemic
90|side-eff ects are urgently needed. Published data3,8 suggest
91|that botulinum toxin A has analgesic eff ects against
92|peripheral neuropathic pain. However, the quality of the
93|evidence is low because it comes mostly from small pilot
94|trials. Findings from one unpublished multicentre study
95|of 117 patients with postherpetic neuralgia9 showed no
96|eff ect of botulinum toxin A compared with placebo on
97|pain intensity from pain diaries (primary outcome), but
98|in view of the inclusion criteria (minimal duration of
99|pain required for inclusion was only 3 months), some
100|patients might have recovered spontaneously. No study
101|has assessed the effi cacy of repeated administrations of
102|botulinum toxin A for the treatment of neuropathic pain.
103|Furthermore, the phenotypic profi les of the patients who
104|respond to the drug have yet to be characterised.

18|Several potential limitations of our study need
19|consideration. One is the moderate sample size; however,
20|our sample met statistical expectations for the primary
21|endpoint, we used several robust statistical models, and
22|we obtained positive results for the primary outcome and
23|several secondary outcomes. Our sample was also highly
24|selected; therefore, the present data should be further
25|investigated in larger settings with fewer exclusion
26|criteria. We used the LOCF method to impute for missing
27|data, which can overestimate effi cacy;31 however, this
28|method is a widespread method of imputation, especially
29|in neuro pathic pain clinical trials,8 and few data were
30|missing in the present study (appendix p 14).

36|appearance, specifi cally wrinkles or colour. We did not
37|photograph our participants' skin during the study, but
38|photographs of a woman who participated in this trial

49|1.4. Supplementary statistical methods
50|1.4.1. Definition of rescue medication
51|Rescue medication was defined as any immunosuppressive medication that would be expected to materially
52|impact efficacy, regardless of whether the medication was used for IgAN. A blinded medical review of
53|concomitant therapy was performed to document which patients were defined as having rescue medication for
54|IgAN or prohibited immunosuppressive medications for non-IgAN indications, and therefore had some or all of
55|their data excluded from the relevant efficacy analyses. When the dose and duration of treatment received was
56|considered unlikely to have impacted all subsequent data, only data at specified visits were excluded. Initiation
57|of dialysis, renal transplantation, and renal-related death (although the latter did not occur) were also included
58|with rescue medication as reasons for exclusion of impacted data from the primary efficacy analyses.

60|1.4.2. Primary efficacy endpoint analysis: time-weighted average of eGFR over 2 years
61|Time-weighted average of eGFR measurements over 2 years were analysed using robust regression, having
62|multiply imputed any missing data first in three phases: an imputation, analysis, and pooling phase. Robust
63|regression was selected because previous eGFR data contained a small sub-population of patients having
64|extreme outlying data resulting from very rapid progression of disease. Robust regression avoids results being
65|unduly influenced by a small subset of patients with outlying data. Data at each individual time point were
66|log-transformed prior to analysis.

69|having imputed 20 datasets separately within each treatment arm. The number of burn-in iterations was set to
70|200, and observations were sampled every 200 iterations within the same chain for each imputed dataset. In the
71|second step of the imputation phase, data at each timepoint were multiply imputed using a regression method

34|1.4.5. Determination of the clinical relevance of the treatment effect on eGFR slope
35|The clinical relevance of the magnitude of the treatment benefit on 2-year eGFR total slope has been assessed by
36|comparison with a threshold derived from the meta-analysis by Inker et al.8 that defined the relationship between
37|eGFR slope and the composite clinical endpoint of kidney failure, eGFR <15 mL/min/1·73 m2 or sustained
38|doubling of serum creatinine.
39|
40|Based on information provided by Inker et al.8 and given the precision with which eGFR total slope has been
41|estimated in this trial, it is calculated that an observed treatment effect of 1·23 mL/min/1·73 m2 per year mean
42|difference in 2-year eGFR total slope would confer >97·5% probability of an effect on the composite clinical
43|endpoint. This study-specific threshold was estimated using the slope, intercept, root mean-squared error, and
44|associated confidence intervals provided by Inker et al.8 for the relationship between 2-year eGFR total slope and
45|log (hazard ratio) for the composite clinical endpoint.
46|
47|1.4.6. Hypothesis testing strategy
48|In order to provide strong control of the type I error rate of 2·5% one-sided across the study, the endpoints of
49|UPCR at 9 months in the first 199 randomised patients, time weighted average of eGFR over 2 years in the
50|overall study population, and 2-year eGFR slope in the overall study population were tested in an endpoint
51|hierarchy. This approach required the primary analysis of UPCR at 9 months to be tested at a one-sided
52|significance level of 0·02, in order to preserve 0·005 for the final analysis of 2-year eGFR in the overall study
53|population in case statistical significance was not achieved for UPCR. Because statistical significance at the
54|required threshold was achieved for the analysis of UPCR at 9 months, the primary time-weighted average of
55|eGFR over 2 years was tested at a one-sided significance level of 0·025. The primary supportive analysis of
56|2-year eGFR total slope was also included in the endpoint hierarchy. As statistical significance was achieved for
57|the time-weighted average of eGFR over 2 years at the one-sided significance level of 0·025, 2-year eGFR total
58|slope was also tested at a one-sided significance level of 0·025.

4|Efficacy and safety of a targeted-release formulation of
5|budesonide in patients with primary IgA nephropathy
6|(NefIgArd): 2-year results from a randomised phase 3 trial
7|Richard Lafayette, Jens Kristensen, Andrew Stone, Jürgen Floege, Vladimir Tesař, Hernán Trimarchi, Hong Zhang, Necmi Eren, Alexander Paliege,
8|Heather N Reich, Brad H Rovin, Jonathan Barratt, on behalf of the NefIgArd trial investigators
9|Summary
10|Background IgA nephropathy is a chronic immune-mediated kidney disease and a major cause of kidney failure
11|worldwide. The gut mucosal immune system is implicated in its pathogenesis, and Nefecon is a novel, oral,
12|targeted-release formulation of budesonide designed to act at the gut mucosal level. We present findings from the
13|2-year, phase 3 NefIgArd trial of Nefecon in patients with IgA nephropathy.
14|Methods In this phase 3, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, adult patients (aged
15|≥18 years) with primary IgA nephropathy, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) 35-90 mL/min per 1·73 m2,
16|and persistent proteinuria (urine protein-creatinine ratio ≥0·8 g/g or proteinuria ≥1 g/24 h) despite optimised renin-
17|angiotensin system blockade were enrolled at 132 hospital-based clinical sites in 20 countries worldwide. Patients
18|were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive 16 mg/day oral capsules of Nefecon or matching placebo for 9 months,
19|followed by a 15-month observational follow-up period off study drug. Randomisation via an interactive response
20|technology system was stratified according to baseline proteinuria (<2 or ≥2 g/24 h), baseline eGFR (<60 or ≥60 mL/min
21|per 1·73 m2), and region (Asia-Pacific, Europe, North America, or South America). Patients, investigators, and site
22|staff were masked to treatment assignment throughout the 2-year trial. Optimised supportive care was also continued
23|throughout the trial. The primary efficacy endpoint was time-weighted average of eGFR over 2 years. Efficacy and
24|safety analyses were done in the full analysis set (ie, all randomly assigned patients). The trial was registered on
25|ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT03643965, and is completed.
26|Findings Patients were recruited to the NefIgArd trial between Sept 5, 2018, and Jan 20, 2021, with 364 patients (182 per
27|treatment group) randomly assigned in the full analysis set. 240 (66%) patients were men and 124 (34%) were women,
28|and 275 (76%) identified as White. The time-weighted average of eGFR over 2 years showed a statistically significant
29|treatment benefit with Nefecon versus placebo (difference 5·05 mL/min per 1·73 m2 [95% CI 3·24 to 7·38], p<0·0001),
30|with a time-weighted average change of -2·47 mL/min per 1·73 m2 (95% CI -3·88 to -1·02) reported with Nefecon
31|and -7·52 mL/min per 1·73 m2 (-8·83 to -6·18) reported with placebo. The most commonly reported treatment-
32|emergent adverse events during treatment with Nefecon were peripheral oedema (31 [17%] patients, vs placebo,
33|seven [4%] patients), hypertension (22 [12%] vs six [3%]), muscle spasms (22 [12%] vs seven [4%]), acne (20 [11%] vs
34|two [1%]), and headache (19 [10%] vs 14 [8%]). No treatment-related deaths were reported.
35|Interpretation A 9-month treatment period with Nefecon provided a clinically relevant reduction in eGFR decline and
36|a durable reduction in proteinuria versus placebo, providing support for a disease-modifying effect in patients with
37|IgA nephropathy. Nefecon was also well tolerated, with a safety profile as expected for a locally acting oral budesonide
38|product.

73|Research in context
74|Evidence before this study
75|The present phase 3 NefIgArd trial was designed and initiated
76|following the findings of the earlier phase 2b NEFIGAN trial by
77|Fellström and colleagues (2017), which reported reduced
78|proteinuria and an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR)
79|treatment benefit favouring Nefecon, a novel, oral,
80|targeted-release capsule formulation of budesonide specifically
81|developed for the treatment of IgAN, versus placebo in
82|patients with IgA nephropathy. All evidence considered before
83|initiating the original phase 2b NEFIGAN trial is described
84|therein. IgA nephropathy is a chronic immune-mediated
85|kidney disease and is a major cause of kidney failure. No
86|effective disease-modifying strategies are available for IgA
87|nephropathy, with treatment recommendations focusing on
88|providing optimised supportive care (including maximally
89|tolerated renin-angiotensin system blockade). Systemic
90|glucocorticoids have been tested in patients with IgA
91|nephropathy, but recent studies (the STOP-IgAN and TESTING
92|trials) have reported mixed efficacy outcomes and increased
93|risk of serious adverse events and of severe infections with
94|their use. A growing body of evidence supports the role of the
95|gut mucosal immune system and mucosal-derived
96|galactose-deficient IgA1 (gd-IgA1) in the pathogenesis of
97|primary IgA nephropathy, with Peyer's patches in the gut
98|identified as a major source of gd-IgA1. Nefecon, developed by
99|Calliditas Therapeutics, is designed to act locally in the distal
100|ileum, where the Peyer's patches are highly concentrated.
101|Separate meta-analyses have shown that reduction in
102|proteinuria and slowing of eGFR decline were predictive of
103|treatment effects on long-term kidney outcomes in chronic
104|kidney disease, including IgA nephropathy. Thus, these markers
105|were used as surrogate endpoints for demonstrating clinical
106|benefit in the phase 3 NefIgArd trial of Nefecon.
107|Added value of this study
108|This full 2-year trial provides evidence that 9 months of
109|treatment with Nefecon 16 mg/day, led to a significant
110|reduction in the average decline in eGFR over 2 years in patients
111|with primary IgA nephropathy, which was preserved during a
112|15-month follow-up period off drug. In addition, a durable
113|reduction in proteinuria was observed for the entire period.
114|Importantly, for the early management of patients with
115|primary IgA nephropathy to delay disease progression, this
116|positive effect of Nefecon on eGFR was independent of baseline
117|proteinuria, and resulted in an approximate 50% reduction in
118|the decline of eGFR compared with placebo over
119|the 2-year period. Nefecon was shown to be well tolerated and
120|had a safety profile that reflected a locally, rather than
121|systemically, acting glucocorticoid product.
122|Implications of all the available evidence
123|Combined with what is known already in the field, the full
124|2-year NefIgArd trial findings provide support to the strategy of
125|targeting the gut-kidney axis in IgA nephropathy. The present
126|results also indicate that treatment with Nefecon 16 mg/day
127|can modify the disease course of IgA nephropathy in a clinically
128|meaningful way and with minimally increased risk of serious
129|adverse events when compared with placebo treatment.

7|Study design
8|NefIgArd was a phase 3, multicentre, randomised,
9|double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. The design and
10|methods have been described in detail previously.8,18 In
11|brief, following a screening period, patients received
12|Nefecon 16 mg/day or matching placebo for a treatment
13|period of 9 months, followed by a 15-month observational
14|follow-up period (including a 2-week tapering period) off
15|study drug. Optimised supportive care (ie, blood pressure
16|control, dietary sodium restriction, and maximally
17|tolerated RAS inhibition) was continued throughout the
18|full 2-year trial. A summary of the trial design is shown
19|in the appendix (p 17).
20|The trial was done at 132 hospital-based clinical sites in
21|20 countries worldwide (appendix p 8). The protocol and
22|informed consent form were approved by each site's
23|institutional review board or independent ethics
24|committee before study initiation. An independent data
25|and safety monitoring board monitored overall study
26|conduct and safety data. The study was conducted in
27|accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and with all
28|applicable laws and regulations of the locale and country

30|International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical

49|Statistical analysis
50|Further details on the statistical analysis methods are
51|included in the appendix (pp 3-5). With 360 patients, the
52|study had 90% power to detect a statistically significant
53|difference in eGFR at 2 years, with use of a one-sided
54|alpha of 2·5%, assuming a difference in mean eGFR of
55|2·24 mL/min per 1·73 m2 at 2 years.17 An endpoint
56|hierarchy was defined to provide control of the type I
57|error rate of 2·5%, one-sided across key endpoints of the
58|study. All p values presented are one-sided, apart from
59|interaction test p values, which are two-sided.
60|Efficacy and safety data were analysed in the full analysis
61|set, which included all randomly assigned patients (apart
62|from two patients who were prospectively excluded due to
63|being incorrectly randomised, and patients recruited for
64|regulatory requirements in China after enrolment of the
65|planned 360 patients was complete).
66|The primary aim of the efficacy analysis was to describe
67|the efficacy of the 9-month regimen of Nefecon 16 mg/day
68|in comparison with placebo, free from the effects of rescue
69|medication and irrespective of any early discontinuation of
70|study treatment. The reasons for exclusion from the
71|primary efficacy analyses of data impacted by rescue
72|medication or prohibited immunosuppressive medications
73|for non-IgA nephropathy indications are described in the
74|appendix (p 3).
75|For the primary efficacy endpoint (time-weighted
76|average of eGFR over 2 years), each timepoint was
77|weighted in proportion to the time elapsed since the
78|previous recording. Therefore, recordings made at
79|18 and 24 months received twice as much weight as those
80|made at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months. The weights totalled 1 so
81|that the treatment effect could be interpreted as the
82|average effect over 2 years. Data were log-transformed
83|before analysis and data included at baseline and
84|24 months were the log of the geometric mean of the two
85|replicate values recorded at each timepoint, respectively.
86|Missing data were imputed by multiple imputation
87|before calculation of each patient's time-weighted
88|average, conditional on their previous eGFR measure
89|ments. Log-transformed time-weighted average values in
90|each imputed dataset were analysed with use of robust
91|regression with log-baseline eGFR fitted as a covariate.
92|Results were combined across imputations according to
93|Rubin's rules.21 Pre-specified sensitivity analyses were
94|done that varied missing data assumptions and that
95|analysed data with a mixed model repeated measures
96|approach. In pre-specified supplementary analyses, the
97|primary analysis was repeated in the per-protocol
98|population (all patients in the full analysis set for whom
99|no major protocol deviations occurred during the study
100|period), and in the full analysis set including data
101|recorded after the use of rescue medication or other
102|prohibited immunosuppressive medications. A post-hoc
103|analysis was also done that excluded data observed after
104|starting SGLT2 inhibitors after baseline (appendix p 6).
105|Time from randomisation to confirmed 30% eGFR
106|reduction or kidney failure was analysed with inverse
107|probability of censoring weights to estimate the effect of
108|Nefecon in the absence of rescue medication over
109|the 2-year trial period. Patients who received rescue
110|medication or other prohibited immunosuppressive
111|medications were censored at the last eGFR recording
112|made before the use of the medications (appendix
113|pp 4-5). Data were analysed using a weighted Cox model
114|with covariates of treatment, log-baseline UPCR,
115|log-baseline eGFR, and geographical region. An
116|unweighted Cox model, with the same covariates, was
117|used in a pre-specified supplementary analysis that
118|additionally
119|counted
120|rescue
121|or
122|other
123|prohibited
124|immunosuppressive medications as an event, and in a
125|post-hoc supplementary analysis that included any data
126|recorded after rescue medication or other prohibited
127|immunosuppressive medications.

42|Discussion
43|In the phase 3 NefIgArd trial, Nefecon treatment led to a
44|sustained and clinically relevant reduction in eGFR
45|decline, supporting the disease-modifying effect of
46|Nefecon in patients with IgA nephropathy. These 2-year
47|results confirm the clinical benefit of Nefecon treatment,
48|the first disease-specific treatment targeting the
49|pathological origin of IgA nephropathy in the GALT. A
50|9-month treatment course of Nefecon 16 mg/day, in
51|addition to optimised RAS inhibition, significantly
52|reduced kidney function decline over 2 years in patients
53|with primary IgA nephropathy, thereby delaying disease
54|progression and prolonging the time to kidney failure.
55|This preservation of kidney function observed over
56|2 years was not necessarily predicted from the previously
57|reported reduction in 1-year proteinuria alone with
58|Nefecon.23 All estimates of the benefit of Nefecon on
59|2-year eGFR slope (appendix p 10) are well above the
60|threshold needed (1·23 mL/min/1·73 m2 per year;
61|appendix p 5) for predicting clinically meaningful effects
62|on the longer term composite endpoint of kidney failure,
63|of eGFR below 15 mL/min per 1·73 m2 or sustained
64|doubling of serum creatinine.24 Indeed, the slowing of
65|kidney function decline observed over this 2-year study
66|led to a significant delay in the composite endpoint of
67|time to confirmed 30% reduction in eGFR or kidney
68|failure in patients who received Nefecon, compared with
69|those who received placebo. Further supporting the
70|overall clinical benefit of Nefecon treatment, potentially
71|as a result of reducing IgA immune complex-mediated
72|inflammation in the glomeruli, the proportion of patients
73|without microhaematuria during observational follow-up
74|was significantly higher in the Nefecon group than in the
75|placebo group.
76|Importantly, the 2-year eGFR treatment effect was
77|consistent across subgroups and was not dependent on
78|baseline proteinuria levels (all interaction tests were non-
79|significant). As expected, the rate of eGFR decline over
80|2 years increased with baseline UPCR levels, evident in
81|analysis of UPCR subgroups. However, irrespective of
82|baseline UPCR, the eGFR treatment effect accumulated
83|with Nefecon by 9 months was sustained throughout the
84|15-month observational follow-up, with no convergence
85|of the eGFR trajectories in patients with lower or higher
86|UPCR levels. This was also indicated in post-hoc analysis
87|of the composite endpoint. Fewer patients with lower
88|UPCR (<1·5 g/g) reached a confirmed 30% reduction in
89|eGFR or kidney failure during the 2-year study, versus
90|patients with higher UPCR (≥1·5 g/g), reflecting the
91|slower underlying rate of eGFR deterioration in patients
92|with lower levels of proteinuria. However, the HRs for
93|the treatment effect of Nefecon versus placebo were
94|similar between the UPCR subgroups.

4|These observations are of clinical importance for the
5|early management of patients who have traditionally been
6|regarded as being at low risk of disease progression, but
7|who are clearly still at substantial risk of developing kidney
8|failure in their lifetime. As lost kidney function cannot be
9|regained, it is desirable to intervene early in the disease
10|course, before substantial GFR decline has occurred. A
11|recent study of patients with primary IgA nephropathy
12|from the UK National Registry of Rare Kidney Diseases6
13|has given insight into the seriousness of the prognosis for
14|patients with IgA nephropathy, including those with low
15|levels of proteinuria. Outcomes were generally poor, with
16|few patients expected to avoid kidney failure in their
17|lifetime.6 Adults in this cohort had a median kidney
18|survival of just 10·8 years (95% CI 10·0-12·0), with a
19|mean age at kidney failure or death of 49 years (SD 14).
20|Among 175 patients with time-averaged UPCR of
21|0·44-0·88 g/g, approximately 30% developed kidney
22|failure within 10 years, and among 215 patients with time-
23|averaged UPCR below 0·44 g/g, this rate was approximately
24|20%.6 Therefore, preserving kidney function earlier rather
25|than later in the disease course is expected to provide the
26|most benefit, when there is more residual kidney function
27|left to protect. Indeed, some parallels can be drawn with
28|rheumatoid arthritis, for which early intervention with
29|disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs is emphasised to
30|reduce inflammation and prevent worsening of the
31|debilitating disease.25
32|The UPCR reduction observed after 9 months of
33|treatment with Nefecon 16 mg/day in the first 199 patients18
34|has been confirmed in this final analysis of all
35|364 patients, and was durable during observational follow-
36|up to 2 years. A maximum reduction of 50% in UPCR was
37|observed in the Nefecon group versus the placebo group
38|at 12 months. At the end of the observational follow-up at
39|2 years, UPCR was reduced by 30% in the Nefecon group
40|versus the placebo group, which was the same magnitude
41|of effect as observed at the end of the 9-month treatment
42|period. This finding suggests that proteinuria might
43|eventually return towards baseline levels, at least in some
44|patients, sometime after the end of a 9-month treatment
45|course. This effect is expected because IgA nephropathy is
46|a chronic immune-mediated disease1 and, although one
47|9-month treatment cycle might offer disease modification
48|and delay disease progression, it is not expected to cure
49|the disease. Thus, re-treatment might be needed for some
50|patients, and the level of proteinuria might potentially
51|guide the timing. However, further research to determine
52|the most appropriate re-treatment strategy is warranted.
53|An ongoing open-label extension study for patients who
54|have completed NefIgArd and have proteinuria levels that
55|meet the same inclusion criteria as NefIgArd could
56|provide valuable additional information on a repeat
57|9-month
58|treatment
59|cycle
60|(NCT04541043).
61|Studies
62|assessing the potential implications of extending the
63|treatment period beyond 9 months might also be
64|warranted.
65|This 2-year analysis also found that the Nefecon safety
66|profile was as expected for a locally acting budesonide
67|product, with low rates of adverse events that are
68|generally mild to moderate in severity and reversible, and
69|not resulting in substantial numbers of patients
70|discontinuing
71|treatment.
72|All
73|four
74|patients
75|who
76|experienced an adverse event of new-onset diabetes
77|during Nefecon treatment were pre-diabetic at study
78|entry, and in all four patients, HbA1c levels decreased
79|after the completion of study treatment, returning to
80|baseline levels in two of the four patients by 3 months
81|after the end of treatment. HbA1c returned to baseline
82|levels in all four patients by 2 years.
83|Severe infections requiring hospitalisation occurred in
84|three patients in the Nefecon group (none considered
85|related to treatment) and one patient in the placebo
86|group. This rate of severe infections is of particular note
87|given that some patients who remain at high risk of
88|disease progression despite optimised supportive care
89|might be considered for a 6-month course of systemic
90|glucocorticoid therapy.7 Current KDIGO guidelines warn
91|of the substantial risk of treatment-emergent toxicity
92|associated with systemic glucocorticoid use, particularly
93|in regard to increased numbers of serious adverse events
94|and severe infections.7 This toxicity has been reported in
95|previous randomised controlled trials in patients with
96|IgA nephropathy treated with systemic glucocorticoids.
97|For instance, in the Therapeutic Effects of Steroids in
98|IgA Nephropathy Global (TESTING) study, the number
99|of patients with incident severe infections requiring
100|hospitalisation was 17 (7%) of 257 in the active treatment
101|group and three (1%) of 246 in the placebo group, despite
102|prophylactic measures.26 In the Supportive Versus
103|Immunosuppressive Therapy for the Treatment of
104|Progressive IgA Nephropathy (STOP-IgAN) study, the
105|number of patients with incident serious infections was
106|eight (10%) of 82 in the active treatment group and
107|three (4%) of 80 in the supportive care group.27 The
108|overall number of patients reporting adverse events that
109|were classed as infections was similar between the
110|Nefecon and placebo groups in the present study, but
111|was higher in the immunosuppression arm of STOP-
112|IgAN27 and was unknown in the TESTING study,26 in
113|which non-serious adverse events were not collected.
114|Other serious or severe complications typically associated
115|with the use of systemic glucocorticoids in similar patient
116|populations were not observed after Nefecon treatment
117|in the present trial.
118|Limitations of this study include the use of surrogate
119|endpoints, rather than long-term clinical outcome
120|measures associated with kidney failure that were not
121|possible to measure over a study duration of 2 years.
122|Nevertheless, as discussed, the eGFR effects observed
123|over 2 years are likely to translate into positive treatment
124|effects on longer term clinical outcome measures.
125|Importantly, we found statistically significant and
126|clinically relevant effects of Nefecon treatment on both
