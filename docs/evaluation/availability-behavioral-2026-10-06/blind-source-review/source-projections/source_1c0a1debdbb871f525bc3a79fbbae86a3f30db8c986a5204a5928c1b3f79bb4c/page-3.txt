14|Statistical Analysis
15|The primary outcome, the occurrence of worsen-
16|ing heart failure or cardiovascular death, was
17|assessed in a time-to-event analysis with the use
18|of a Cox proportional-hazards model, stratified
19|according to diabetes status. This analysis was
20|performed concurrently in the overall population
21|and in patients with a left ventricular ejection
22|fraction of less than 60%, with an alpha level of
23|0.024 used in the former analysis and an alpha
24|level of 0.038 used in the latter analysis (see the
25|Supplementary Methods section and Fig. S1 and
26|Table S2). We estimated that enrollment of 6100
27|patients followed for at least 13.5 months (and
28|up to 39 months) would result in the occurrence
29|of at least 1117 events and would provide the
30|trial with 93% power to detect a hazard ratio of
31|0.80 for the comparison of dapagliflozin and
32|placebo with respect to the primary outcome in
33|the overall population, at a two-sided alpha level
34|of 0.024. All the analyses were performed ac-
35|cording to the intention-to-treat principle. Second-
36|ary analyses were performed with the use of a
37|closed-testing procedure that included a pre-
38|specified hierarchical ordering of the primary
39|and secondary outcomes; these outcomes in-
40|cluded (in hierarchical order) the total number
41|of worsening heart failure events and cardiovas-
42|cular deaths, a decrease in symptom burden as
43|measured by an increase in the KCCQ total
44|symptom score, and cardiovascular death and
45|death from any cause (both of which were as-
46|sessed in a time-to-event analysis). We analyzed
47|the KCCQ total symptom score as a composite
48|outcome based on the rank of the change in
49|score from baseline to month 8, with a corre-
50|sponding win ratio used to estimate the magni-
51|tude of the treatment effect.11-13 We assessed the
52|consistency of the treatment effect on the pri-
53|mary outcome in prespecified subgroups. In
54|separate sensitivity analyses, patient data were
55|censored at the time of coronavirus disease 2019
56|(Covid-19) diagnosis, and death from noncardio-
57|vascular causes was taken into account as a
58|competing risk.14
59|Results
60|Patients
61|Between August 27, 2018, and December 30,
62|2020, a total of 10,418 patients were screened at
63|353 centers in 20 countries; of these patients,
64|6263 were randomly assigned to receive dapa-
65|gliflozin or matching placebo (Fig. S2). The
66|reasons for exclusion from randomization are
67|provided in Table S3. The demographic and
68|clinical characteristics of the two groups were
69|well balanced at baseline (Table 1 and Table S4).
70|Dapagliflozin was discontinued for reasons other
71|than death in 444 patients (14.2%), and placebo
72|was discontinued for reasons other than death
73|in 442 patients (14.1%). The vital status was
74|known at the end of the trial in all but 2 patients
75|in the dapagliflozin group and 2 patients in the
76|placebo group. The median duration of follow-
77|up was 2.3 years (interquartile range, 1.7 to 2.8).
78|Efficacy
79|In the overall population, the primary outcome
80|occurred in 512 patients (16.4%) in the dapa-
81|gliflozin group and in 610 patients (19.5%) in
82|the placebo group (hazard ratio, 0.82; 95% con-
83|fidence interval [CI], 0.73 to 0.92; P<0.001) (Ta-
84|ble 2 and Fig. 1A). The results of the analysis of
85|the primary outcome in the patients with a left
86|ventricular ejection fraction of less than 60%
87|were similar to those of the overall population
88|(hazard ratio, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.73 to 0.95;
89|P = 0.009) (Table S5).
