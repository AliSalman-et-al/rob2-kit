22|Discussion
23|In this randomized, placebo-controlled trial in-
24|volving patients with heart failure and a mildly
25|reduced or preserved ejection fraction, dapa-
26|gliflozin resulted in a lower risk of the primary
27|composite outcome, worsening heart failure or
28|cardiovascular death, than placebo, with no ap-
29|preciable difference in benefit among patients
30|with a left ventricular ejection fraction of 60% or
31|more and those with a left ventricular ejection
32|fraction of less than 60%, or in other subgroups.
33|Each of the three components of this composite
34|outcome was less common in the dapagliflozin
35|group than in the placebo group. In addition,
36|dapagliflozin resulted in fewer total worsening
37|heart failure events and cardiovascular deaths
38|and a lower symptom burden than placebo. The
39|incidence of adverse events was similar to that in
40|the placebo group.
41|In a previous trial (DAPA-HF; Dapagliflozin
42|and Prevention of Adverse Outcomes in Heart
43|Failure), dapagliflozin reduced the risk of wors-
44|ening heart failure or cardiovascular death
45|among patients with heart failure and a left
46|ventricular ejection fraction of 40% or less.1 The
47|results of the DELIVER trial extend those of the
48|DAPA-HF trial to patients with heart failure and
49|a left ventricular ejection fraction of more than
50|40% and are consistent with the overall results
51|of the EMPEROR-Preserved trial (Empagliflozin
52|Outcome Trial in Patients with Chronic Heart
53|Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction), which
54|assessed the effects of empagliflozin in patients
55|with a left ventricular ejection fraction of more
56|than 40%.10 The rationale for the dual primary
57|analyses in our trial (i.e., evaluation of the pri-
58|mary outcome in patients with a left ventricular
59|ejection fraction of less than 60% in addition to
60|the overall patient population) was based on
61|concern about a potential declining benefit in
62|patients with an ejection fraction in the normal
63|range that had been observed in several previous
64|trials of neurohormonal modulators.6,15 Although
65|the EMPEROR-Preserved trial suggested some
66|potential attenuation of benefit in the highest part
67|of the range of ejection fraction,8 we observed no
68|evidence of heterogeneity with respect to left
69|ventricular ejection fraction in the DELIVER trial,
70|with similar overall treatment effects among
71|patients with a left ventricular ejection fraction
72|of 60% or more and those with a left ventricular
73|ejection fraction of less than 60%. This finding
74|suggests that the benefit of SGLT2 inhibition is
75|likely to extend throughout the full range of
76|ejection fraction.
77|The DELIVER trial was designed with broader
78|inclusion criteria than those used in previous
79|trials involving similar populations in that we en-
80|rolled patients who were hospitalized or recently
81|hospitalized, for whom evidence-based therapy
82|is limited, as well as those with heart failure and
83|a left ventricular ejection fraction that had im-
84|proved to more than 40% at the time of enroll-
85|ment.4 Our data suggest that these understudied
86|groups also benefit from dapagliflozin.
87|The most recent guidelines of the American
88|Heart Association, American College of Cardiol-
89|ogy, and Heart Failure Society of America desig-
90|nated SGLT2 inhibitors as class IIA, level B, for
91|the treatment of heart failure with a mildly re-
92|duced or preserved left ventricular ejection frac-
93|tion.4 The results of the DELIVER trial may in-
94|form future guidelines and provide further
95|guidance for their broader use in clinical prac-
