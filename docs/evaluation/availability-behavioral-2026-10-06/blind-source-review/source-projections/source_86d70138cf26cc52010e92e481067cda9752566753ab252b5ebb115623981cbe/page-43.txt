8|Disease, problem or condition
9|under investigation
10|Heart failure with mildly reduced or preserved ejection fraction
11|(HFmrEF or HFpEF)
12|Sex and gender
13|With increasing ejection fraction in heart failure, the proportion of
14|women is increased, with a higher proportion of men with heart
15|failure and LVEF < 50% and a higher proportion of women with
16|heart failure and LVEF > 60%.
17|Race or ethnic group
18|In the United States, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction is
19|rising in prevalence in Black patients1.
20|Geography
21|Heart failure with mildly reduced or preserved ejection fraction
22|affects patients globally, with some differences in phenotypic
23|presentation in different locations2.
24|Other considerations
25|Heart failure with mildly reduced ejection fraction (LVEF between
26|40 and 49%) is generally similar phenotypically to heart failure with
27|reduced ejection fraction.
28|Overall representativeness of this
29|trial
30|The participants in the present trial demonstrated the expected
31|ratio of men to women based on the ejection fraction distribution.
32|The proportion of women is expectedly slightly lower than in prior
33|HFpEF trials in which the LVEF inclusion criteria were higher.
34|Biologic sex was reported by the participants. The age distribution
35|in this trial is typical for heart failure with mildly reduced or
36|preserved ejection fraction, and similar to other trials in this
37|population. The proportion of Black patients who underwent
38|randomization overall was small (2.5%), primarily because DELIVER
39|was a global trial and the population distribution of Black people in
40|the majority of countries in which DELIVER enrolled was very low.
41|Among patients enrolled in the United States, 14.3% were Black,
42|which is slightly larger than the population distribution of Black
43|people in the United States (12.4%). The causes of heart failure and
44|the coexisting conditions, including kidney function, were
45|consistent with the epidemiology and registry data available from
46|participating countries. No patients were enrolled in Africa.
