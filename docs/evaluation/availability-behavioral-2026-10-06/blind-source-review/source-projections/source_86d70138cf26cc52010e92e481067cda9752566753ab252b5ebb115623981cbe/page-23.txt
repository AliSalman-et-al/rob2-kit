18|Dual Primary Endpoint Analysis and Allocation of Alpha
19|
20|Statistical significance was assessed in two branches in the pre-specified hierarchical order of the endpoints and
21|populations as specified in Figure S1, controlling the Type I error at an overall two-sided 5% level across primary and
22|secondary endpoints, accounting for the planned interim analysis. The total significance level, alpha, was split for the
23|two primary analyses of the primary endpoint, allocating α1 to test the subpopulation and α2 to test the full population.
24|For derivation of the two-sided nominal p-value thresholds α1 and α2, a two-sided alpha of 0.2% was allocated to the
25|interim analysis and 4.8% to the final analysis. The two-sided significance level α2 (for the primary analysis in the full
26|population) was fixed at 2.4%. The inherent correlation structure between the full population and the LVEF < 60%
27|subpopulation, where the corresponding test statistics for the primary endpoint are bivariate normal with correlation
28|equal to the proportion of events in the LVEF < 60% subpopulation, was taken into account when calculating α1 9. The
29|correlation was based on the square root of the lower bound of a two-sided 95% confidence interval for the proportion
30|of events in the subpopulation with LVEF < 60%, using a normal approximation confidence interval for the proportion.
31|The threshold α1 was such that for α2 = 2.4% two-sided; the two-sided probability of rejecting at least one true null
32|hypothesis at the final analysis was 4.8%. Table S2 shows how the two-sided nominal p-value threshold α1 depends on
33|the proportion of events in the LVEF < 60% subpopulation at the final analysis.
