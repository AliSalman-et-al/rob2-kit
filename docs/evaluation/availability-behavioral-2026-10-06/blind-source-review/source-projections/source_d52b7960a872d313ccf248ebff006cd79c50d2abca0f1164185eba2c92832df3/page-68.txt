9|Important medical event or medical treatment
10|Medical and scientific judgement should be exercised in deciding whether a case is serious in
11|situations where important medical events may not be immediately life threatening or result in
12|death, hospitalisation, disability or incapacity but may jeopardize the subject or may require
13|medical treatment to prevent one or more outcomes listed in the definition of serious. These should
14|usually be considered as serious.
15|Simply stopping the suspect drug does not mean that it is an important medical event; medical
16|judgement must be used.

33|mild (awareness of sign or symptom, but easily tolerated)

35|moderate (discomfort sufficient to cause interference with normal activities)

38|It is important to distinguish between serious and severe AEs. Severity is a measure of intensity
39|whereas seriousness is defined by the criteria in Appendix B 2. An AE of severe intensity need
40|not necessarily be considered serious. For example, nausea that persists for several hours may be
41|considered severe nausea, but not a SAE unless it meets the criteria shown in Appendix B 2. On
42|the other hand, a stroke that results in only a limited degree of disability may be considered a mild
43|stroke but would be a SAE when it satisfies the criteria shown in Appendix B 2.
