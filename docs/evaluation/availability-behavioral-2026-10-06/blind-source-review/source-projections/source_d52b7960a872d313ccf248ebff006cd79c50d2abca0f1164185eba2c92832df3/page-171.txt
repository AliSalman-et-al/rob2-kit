15|Definition of StroGe and Transient Ischemic AttacG
16|The distinction between an Ischemic Stroke and a Transient Ischemic Attack is the presence of
17|infarction. Persistence of symptoms U24 hours or until death3 is an acceptable indicator of acute
18|infarction in the absence of imaging evidence of infarction.
19|Transient Ischemic AttacG
20|Transient ischemic attack HTIAI is defined as a transient episode of focal neurological dysfunction
21|caused by brain, spinal cord, or retinal ischemia, without acute infarction.
22|StroGe
23|Stroke is defined as an acute episode of focal or global neurological dysfunction caused by brain, spinal
24|cord, or retinal vascular injury as a result of haemorrhage or infarction.
25|Classification:
26|A. Ischemic StroGe
27|Ischemic stroke is defined as an acute episode of focal cerebral, spinal, or retinal dysfunction caused
28|by infarction of central nervous system tissue.
29|Haemorrhage may be a consequence of ischemic stroke. In this situation, the stroke is an ischemic
30|stroke with haemorrhagic transformation and not a haemorrhagic stroke.
31|B. Haemorrhagic StroGe
32|Haemorrhagic stroke is defined as an acute episode of focal or global cerebral or spinal dysfunction
33|caused by non-traumatic intraparenchymal, intraventricular, or subarachnoid haemorrhage. NOTED
34|Subdural hematomas are intracranial haemorrhagic events and not strokes.
35|C. Undetermined StroGe
36|Lndetermined stroke is defined as an acute episode of focal or global neurological dysfunction caused by
37|presumed brain, spinal cord, or retinal vascular injury as a result of haemorrhage or infarction but with
38|insufficient information to allow categorization as either ischemic or haemorrhagic.
