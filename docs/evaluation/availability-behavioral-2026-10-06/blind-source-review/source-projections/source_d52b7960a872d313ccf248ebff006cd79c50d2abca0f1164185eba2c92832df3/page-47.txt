22|8.5
23|Collection of adverse events
24|The Principal Investigator is responsible for ensuring that all staff involved in the study are familiar
25|with the content of this section.
26|The definitions of an AE or SAE can be found in Appendix B.
27|AE will be reported by the patient (or, when appropriate, by a caregiver, surrogate, or the patient's
28|legally authorised representative).
29|The Investigator and any designees are responsible for detecting, documenting, and recording
30|events that meet the definition of a SAEs and DAEs, amputation and events potentially placing the
31|patient at risk for a lower limb amputation (preceding events). For information on how to follow-
32|up AEs see Section 8.5.3.
33|8.5.1
34|Method of detecting AEs and SAEs
35|Care will be taken not to introduce bias when detecting SAEs or DAEs. Open-ended and
36|non-leading verbal questioning of the patient is the preferred method to inquire about
37|AE occurrences.
38|Safety information on SAEs and DAEs, amputations, adverse events (AEs) leading to amputation
39|and potential risk factor AEs for amputations affecting lower limbs will be collected and entered
40|into eCRFs by site personnel according to the study visit schedule.
41|If the potential efficacy event fulfils SAE criteria (see Appendix B 2) the site is to record and report
42|these events to the Sponsor or designee within timelines described in Section 8.6.1.
