25|Justification for dose
26|The 10 mg dose of dapagliflozin has a well-characterised efficacy and safety profile in the T2D
27|clinical development program and is the recommended dose in the majority of countries
28|worldwide.
29|From a pharmacokinetic perspective, the currently approved dapagliflozin dose of 10 mg once
30|daily is appropriate for use in patients with HFpEF. Slightly higher systemic exposure to
31|dapagliflozin is expected in HFpEF patients when symptomatic, based on the dual renal and
32|hepatic metabolism of dapagliflozin and the lower perfusion of these organs in this patient group.
33|However, the increase in systemic exposure of 10 mg dapagliflozin is not anticipated to warrant
34|dose adjustment in HF patients. Moreover, the anticipated slightly higher systemic exposure to
35|dapagliflozin is likely to be beneficial in HF patients, by compensating for the reduced renal
36|perfusion and consequently lower renal glucose and sodium filtered loads in these patients. Doses
37|lower than 10 mg are therefore unlikely to provide as much benefit to patients with HF as the
38|10-mg dose. Lastly, no changes in dose of concomitant medications in the HFpEF population are
39|needed due to a lack of clinically meaningful drug-drug interactions for dapagliflozin with current
40|medications used for treatment of patients with HFpEF, including standard of care medications
41|used to control co-morbidities in this patient group.
42|In the dapagliflozin clinical program, there are no dose-related SAEs that preclude the use of
43|10 mg as a preferred dose. Additionally, in a post-hoc analysis of data from 320 patients with
44|a documented history of HF and concomitant T2D in placebo-controlled clinical trials,
45|dapagliflozin 10 mg was found to be well tolerated in this population (Kosiborod et al 2017b).
