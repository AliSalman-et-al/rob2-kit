11|LABELLING AND SHIPMENT OF BIOHAZARD SAMPLES
12|International Airline Transportation Association (IATA) classifies biohazardous agents into 3
13|categories (http://www.iata.org/whatwedo/cargo/dangerous_goods/infectious_substances.htm).
14|For transport purposes the classification of infectious substances according to risk groups was
15|removed from the Dangerous Goods Regulations in the 46th edition (2005). Infectious
16|substances are now classified either as Category A, Category B or Exempt. There is no direct
17|relationship between Risk Groups and Categories A and B.
18|Category A Infectious Substances are infectious substances in a form that, when exposure to it
19|occurs, is capable of causing permanent disability, life-threatening or fatal disease in otherwise
20|healthy humans or animals. Category A pathogens are e.g., Ebola, Lassa fever virus:
