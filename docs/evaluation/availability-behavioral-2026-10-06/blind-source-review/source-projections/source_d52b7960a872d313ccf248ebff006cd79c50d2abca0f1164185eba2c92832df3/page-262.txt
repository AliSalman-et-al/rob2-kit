13|rare occasionsO the 95C confidence interval for WR could exclude unity while the p-value for
14|the pre-planned hypothesis test could be R 0.05O or the hypothesis test could be < 0.05 with the

18|The win ratio represents the odds of having a more favourable outcome versus a less
19|favourable outcome when assigned to the dapaglifloIin 10 mg treatment group as opposed to
20|placebo. More specificallyO each patient in the dapaglifloIin group is compared with each
21|patient in the placebo group and each pair is labelled as EwinnerFO EloserFO or EtieFO depending
22|on whether the patient on dapaglifloIin has a more favourableO less favourableO or the same
23|outcomeO respectivelyO with respect to the composite ranGed endpoint compared to the patient
24|on placebo. Win ratio is defined as the ratio of the number of EwinnerF pairs to the number of
25|EloserF pairs for the dapaglifloIin arm. If the estimated win ratio is greater than 1 then the
26|treatment effect is in favour of dapaglifloIin.
27|The win ratio statistic adjusted for the randomisation stratification factor and baseline KCCQ-
28|TSS will be obtained using the methodology in (Koch et al 1998O Kawaguchi et al 2011) for
29|the stratified Mann-Whitney estimators for the comparison of two treatments with
30|randomisation based covariance adjustment. The win ratio statistic will be calculated as
31|Mann-Whitney oddsO ieO WR S MW \ (1 W MW)O where MW is the adjusted Mann-Whitney
32|estimate. This transformation is monotonous in the domain of the Mann-Whitney estimate.
33|The 95C confidence interval for the win ratio will be obtained by transforming the bounds of
34|the confidence interval (Koch et al 1998) for the Mann-Whitney estimateO using the same
35|transformation as for the win ratio.
