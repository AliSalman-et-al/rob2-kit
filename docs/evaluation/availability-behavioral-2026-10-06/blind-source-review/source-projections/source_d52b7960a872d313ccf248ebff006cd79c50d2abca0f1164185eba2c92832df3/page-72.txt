33|Transient Ischemic Attack
34|Transient ischemic attack (TIA) is defined as a transient episode of focal neurological dysfunction
35|caused by brain, spinal cord, or retinal ischemia, without acute infarction.
36|
37|Stroke
38|Stroke is defined as an acute episode of focal or global neurological dysfunction caused by brain, spinal
39|cord, or retinal vascular injury as a result of haemorrhage or infarction.
40|
41|Classification:
42|A. Ischemic Stroke
43|Ischemic stroke is defined as an acute episode of focal cerebral, spinal, or retinal dysfunction caused
44|by infarction of central nervous system tissue.
45|Haemorrhage may be a consequence of ischemic stroke. In this situation, the stroke is an ischemic
46|stroke with haemorrhagic transformation and not a haemorrhagic stroke.
47|B. Haemorrhagic Stroke
48|Haemorrhagic stroke is defined as an acute episode of focal or global cerebral or spinal dysfunction
49|caused by non-traumatic intraparenchymal, intraventricular, or subarachnoid haemorrhage. NOTE:
50|Subdural hematomas are intracranial haemorrhagic events and not strokes.
