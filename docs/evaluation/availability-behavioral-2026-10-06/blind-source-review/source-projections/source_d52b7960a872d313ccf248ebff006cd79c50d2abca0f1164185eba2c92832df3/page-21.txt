8|2.3.1
9|Potential risks to patients
10|Dapagliflozin reduces blood volume and blood pressure from its diuretic effect, which could be
11|a concern in patients with HFpEF, but also be important mechanisms of a potential treatment
12|effect. However, in the dapagliflozin type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D) program, the rate of events
13|related to volume depletion and impaired renal function have been similar between dapagliflozin
14|and placebo. Loop-diuretics are widely used in the target patient population and are also allowed
15|in this study. A pooled analysis of patients with T2D and HF in the dapagliflozin development
16|program, showed no increase of volume depletion events but increase in renal events, mainly
17|creatinine increases, in patients treated with dapagliflozin (n=171) compared with placebo treated
18|patients (n=149). About half of the patients were on loop diuretics (Kosiborod et al 2017b).
19|An increase in amputations, mostly affecting toes, was observed in a clinical trial (Neal et al 2017)
20|with another SGLT2 inhibitor. There is no indication from the clinical development program that
21|dapagliflozin is associated with an increased risk of amputation (see Section 8.5.1.1 for the
22|detection and capture of amputation events).
23|Dapagliflozin has not been shown to induce hypoglycaemia in non-diabetes patients. In clinical
24|pharmacology studies, healthy subjects have been treated with single oral doses up to 500 mg and
25|multiple oral doses of 100 mg up to 14 days without any hypoglycaemic events.
26|There have been post-marketing reports of ketoacidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis, in
27|patients with T2D taking dapagliflozin and other SGLT2 inhibitors, although a causal relationship
28|has not been established.
29|Patients treated with dapagliflozin who present with signs and symptoms consistent with
30|ketoacidosis, including nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, malaise, and shortness of breath, should
31|be assessed for ketoacidosis, even if blood glucose levels are below 14 mmol/L (250 mg/dL). If
32|ketoacidosis is suspected interruption of dapagliflozin treatment should be considered and the
33|patient should be promptly evaluated.
34|Predisposing factors to ketoacidosis include a low beta-cell function reserve resulting from
35|pancreatic disorders (e.g., T1D, history of pancreatitis, or pancreatic surgery), insulin dose
36|reduction, reduced caloric intake, or increased insulin requirements due to infections, illness or
37|surgery and alcohol abuse. Dapagliflozin should be used with caution in patients in these
38|circumstances. Dapagliflozin is currently not indicated for the treatment of patients with T1D;
39|these patients are excluded from this study.
40|2.3.1.1
41|Protection against risks
42|This study has been designed with appropriate measures in place to monitor and minimise any
43|potential risks to participating patients. Data regarding amputations and adverse events potentially
44|placing the patient at risk for a lower limb amputation will be collected (see Section 8.5.1.1). To
45|ensure the safety of all patients participating in AstraZeneca sponsored studies, reviews of all
46|safety information from all ongoing clinical dapagliflozin studies are conducted as they become
47|available. In addition, an independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will be responsible for
48|safeguarding the interests of the patients by reviewing safety data throughout the study (see Section
49|9.5.1).
