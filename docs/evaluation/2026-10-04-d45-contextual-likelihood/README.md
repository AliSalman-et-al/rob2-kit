# D4.5: permit contextual inference about likely influence

## Demonstrated rule mismatch

Production operational guidance required evidence that influence occurred or strong beliefs plus a judgment opportunity. That makes the strong-belief example in Box 10 an exhaustive evidential requirement. Official Cochrane RoB 2 (22 August 2019), section 7.3 pp.52–53 also instructs assessors to consider conscious and subconscious interests and expectations through comparator, assessor involvement in care, other actors/interviewers and assessment timing. The clinician-decision discussion on p.52 describes likely influence without requiring measured belief strength. These routes permit a calibrated probable judgment without direct proof of changed ratings or documented strong beliefs.

The correction replaces that restrictive criterion in the existing operational card, rather than adding another prompt. It supplies concern and reassurance factors, keeps possible influence separate from likely influence, and explicitly prevents absence of documented influence/beliefs alone from deciding the answer. Operational guidance version is 1.0.11. Official question text/excerpt, choices, activation, deterministic defaults and evidence validation remain unchanged. NI remains valid when contextual evidence cannot support a likelihood judgment. This is rule fidelity, not demonstrated answer accuracy or benchmark improvement.

## Remaining production audit

Allowed answers and question dependencies match the relevant official Boxes/tables: D2.4 follows affirmative D2.3; D2.5 follows Y/PY/NI D2.4; D2.7 follows N/PN/NI D2.6; D3.2 excludes NI; D3.3 follows negative D3.2; D3.4 follows Y/PY/NI D3.3; D4.3–4.5 preserve NI-conditioned reasoning; all D5 questions are unconditional. Reviewed default domain mappings agree with Tables 3/4, 6, 10, 12 and 14. Previously corrected overall aggregation and D3.2 No behavior were inspected, not reimplemented. Definitive-answer unresolved-premise controls concern material question premises; no additional exception or general relaxation is justified here.

A separate architectural limitation remains: Cochrane p.4 and each decision table describe proposed judgments that assessors may override with reasons. `DomainDraft` exposes no adopted-label field; `save_domain_judgment` always commits `evaluate_domain`, and artifact verification independently replays default drivers. Thus it cannot represent a defensible nondefault judgment without changing signaling answers. Do not fix this by bending answers. The falsifiable alternative is a versioned, optional Result/domain/answer-bound adjudication recording proposed/adopted labels, source-bound reasons and authority. Test unchanged answer vectors with/without a justified override, invalid/foreign/stale bindings, deterministic historical replay and tamper rejection through both artifact verifiers. An implementation touching only the save endpoint would be incomplete. This cycle does not introduce an unverified override or arbitrary label escape hatch.
