**Domain 5 judgment: Some concerns.** For this exact randomized result, **5.1 = No information; 5.2 = No information; 5.3 = No information.** The supplied evidence leaves prespecification and selection unresolved; it does not establish results-driven selection.

|---|---|---|
| **5.1: Analysis followed a plan finalized before unblinded outcomes were available?** | **No information** | The article reports the first-event composite definition, ITT endpoint analysis, categorical comparison methods and person-time rate ratios (article **p. 3, lines 46–57, 70–87**). It mentions an earlier design publication and registration (**p. 2, lines 40–43**), but that publication’s contents and a dated, applicable analysis plan are not supplied. The registry’s first posting was September 2005 and its latest update was March 2011 (registry **p. 1, lines 243–254**); these do not establish plan finalization or investigators’ access to unblinded outcomes. Neither an early nor a late analysis plan can be established. |
| **5.2: Selection from multiple eligible outcome measurements based on results?** | **No information** | The article defines and reports the four-component first-event composite at two years (article **p. 3, lines 46–57; p. 4, lines 31–48**). The supplied registry lists mortality, MI and unstable angina separately at two years, without explicitly listing stroke or the composite (registry **p. 1, lines 155–160**). This leaves the intended measurement set and correspondence unresolved. Different composite definitions or assessment times could be eligible, but their intended eligibility is not established. The discrepancy does not show that the reported composite was chosen because of its results. |
| **5.3: Selection from multiple eligible analyses based on results?** | **No information** | The article describes categorical tests and ITT person-time rate ratios (article **p. 3, lines 78–87**), while the exact randomized result is reported as **52/499 versus 54/502, P=0.87** (**p. 4, lines 42–48**). These descriptions leave possible analysis choices, including cumulative-risk versus person-time approaches, insufficiently resolved. No detailed prospective intentions or evidence of results-driven choice is supplied. No full-population RR/OR is reported here; subgroup and observational estimates cannot substitute for it. |

**Firm evidence:** the randomized groups contain 499 clopidogrel and 502 aspirin participants, all followed for two years, and the full-population composite result is explicitly reported (article **p. 4, lines 31–48**). The below-median vWF/platelet-count findings are explicitly identified as post hoc and outside the primary aims (**p. 7, lines 103–108**). Those findings do not establish selection of this full-population randomized result.

**Reasonable inference:** the reported result is consistent with the article’s stated first-event endpoint, two-year follow-up and ITT approach. That consistency supports correspondence with the published methods, but cannot establish prospective timing or complete reporting of eligible alternatives.

**Unresolved information:** the applicable plan’s detail and finalization date, investigator access to unblinded outcomes, historical registry content, and the intended eligible measurements and analyses. The trial was open with platelet-test results blinded (**p. 2, lines 23–27**); confidential DSMB database access (**p. 4, lines 18–24**) does not itself establish investigator access for analysis decisions.

These gaps support **Some concerns**, rather than Low risk. They do **not** support High risk: there is no supplied evidence that this exact numerical result was selected on the basis of results.
