46|Abstract
47|Aim: To determine the efficacy and safety of once-weekly dulaglutide added to basal
48|insulin in Chinese patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) with inadequate
49|glycaemic control.
50|Materials and Methods: In the phase III, double-blind AWARD-CHN3 study, Chinese
51|patients with T2DM (N = 291) and glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) ≥7.0% and
52|≤11.0% receiving stable doses of basal insulin glargine with metformin and/or acar-
53|bose were randomized (1:1) to receive add-on dulaglutide 1.5 mg once weekly or pla-
54|cebo once weekly. The primary endpoint was the superiority of dulaglutide/glargine
55|to placebo/glargine for change from baseline in HbA1c at Week 28.
56|Results: The least squares (LS) mean ± standard error change in HbA1c from baseline
57|to Week 28 was 2.0 ± 0.08% with dulaglutide/glargine and 1.1 ± 0.07% with pla-
58|cebo/glargine (LS mean difference: 1.0%, 95% confidence interval [CI] -1.1 to
59| 0.8; P < 0.001), and more patients receiving dulaglutide/glargine achieved HbA1c
60|levels <7.0% (75.9% vs. 33.8%; P < 0.001 vs. placebo/glargine). Body weight
61|decreased with dulaglutide/glargine and increased with placebo/glargine (LS mean
62|difference: 1.2 kg, 95% CI -1.8 to - 0.6; P < 0.001). Reductions in fasting serum
63|glucose were greater with dulaglutide/glargine than with placebo/glargine (LS mean
64|difference: 0.8 mmol/L, 95% CI -1.1 to - 0.5; P < 0.001). The incidence of hypogly-
65|caemia was similar with dulaglutide/glargine and placebo/glargine (29.2% vs. 31.3%;
66|P = 0.704); no patient in either group had severe hypoglycaemia. The most common
67|treatment-emergent adverse events with dulaglutide/glargine were decreased appe-
68|tite (22.2%), diarrhoea (13.2%) and nausea (10.4%).
69|Conclusions: Dulaglutide added to basal insulin was efficacious and well tolerated in
70|Chinese patients with T2DM.

5|INTRODUCTION
6|China has the largest number of individuals living with diabetes of
7|any country in the world, accounting for one in four ( 141 million)
8|of the approximately 537 million adults (20-79 years old) with diabe-
9|tes globally in 2021.1 Furthermore, the number of Chinese people
10|with diabetes is currently increasing, and is predicted to reach
11|174 million by 2045.2 Among all people with diabetes, approxi-
12|mately 90% have type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), both worldwide1
13|and in China.3 Exogenous basal insulin is used by a large proportion
14|of Chinese patients with T2DM to manage their hyperglycaemia,4
15|and often provides initial improvements in glycaemic control. How-
16|ever, T2DM is a progressive disease and most patients require upti-
17|tration of their insulin dose to continue achieving glycaemic
18|control.5,6 Insulin dose titration is often inadequate, with key bar-
19|riers to uptitration in Asia and China including fear of hypoglycaemia
20|and weight gain.7 In clinical trials of exogenous insulin therapy, only
21|25% to 70% of patients achieve glycaemic control.8-12 In real-world
22|settings in Asia, including China, the proportion of patients with
23|T2DM achieving glycaemic control with basal insulin is as low as
24|40%.13-15 Therefore, there is a need to continually establish more
25|effective treatment strategies for T2DM.
26|Combining a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist 1 (GLP-
27|1RA) with basal insulin is a guideline-recommended treatment strat-
28|egy, and this approach exerts complementary effects on glycaemic
29|control and body weight.16 In particular, the combination of a once-
30|weekly GLP-1RA with basal insulin permits a lower number of injec-
31|tions compared with a once-daily GLP-1RA. Furthermore, the flexible
32|dosing afforded by this approach, with each component administered
33|separately, allows the requirements of individual patients to be more
34|closely matched than with fixed-ratio combinations of basal insulin/
35|GLP-1RA.
36|In the phase III AWARD-9 study, dulaglutide 1.5 mg once
37|weekly added to basal insulin glargine significantly improved gly-
38|caemic control, reduced body weight and reduced insulin dose
39|without increasing the risk of hypoglycaemia compared with pla-
40|cebo.17 This indicates that the combination of a GLP-1RA with
41|basal insulin is an effective and well-tolerated therapeutic option
42|in a predominantly White population with T2DM.17 However,
43|there is still a lack of data regarding the use of a long-acting GLP-
44|1RA once weekly added to basal insulin in Chinese patients with
45|T2DM. This is a particularly relevant knowledge gap for Chinese
46|patients given the large number of individuals living with T2DM in
47|China. Therefore, in the AWARD-CHN3 study, we aimed to inves-
48|tigate the effect of once-weekly dulaglutide added to basal insulin
49|on glycaemic control, body weight and relevant safety outcomes in
50|Chinese patients with T2DM.

54|2.1

56|Study design and participants
57|The
58|phase
59|III,
60|multicentre,
61|randomized,
62|double-blind,
63|placebo-
64|controlled AWARD-CHN3 study included adults (≥18 years of age)
65|with T2DM (according to the World Health Organization diagnostic
66|criteria) with HbA1c levels ≥7.0% and ≤11.0% who had been receiving
67|a stable dose of basal insulin glargine once daily with unchanged
68|doses of metformin and/or acarbose for at least 3 months prior to ini-
69|tial screening. The insulin glargine dose was considered stable when
70|all doses during this period were within the range defined by ±20% of
71|the most commonly used insulin dose during the same period.
72|Patients were also required to have had a stable body weight (±5%)
73|for at least 3 months prior to screening, to have a body mass index
74|(BMI) 19.0 to 35.0 kg/m2 and to be able and willing to take the study
75|drug, including self-injection, and to self-monitor blood glucose. Key
76|exclusion criteria included type 1 diabetes mellitus, pancreatitis, and
77|serum calcitonin level ≥20 pg/mL.

80|Procedures
81|The study comprised three periods: a screening/lead-in period
82|(3 weeks); a treatment period (28 weeks); and a safety follow-up
83|period (4 weeks; Figure S1). During the screening/lead-in period, eligi-
84|ble patients were identified and continued their stable dose of insulin
85|and oral antidiabetic drugs (OADs). During the lead-in period, gluc-
86|ometers were provided and patients were trained to perform self-
87|monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG) and weekly insulin assessments
88|according to the treat-to-target (TTT) algorithm (Table S1). However,
89|insulin dose adjustments based on the TTT algorithm were permitted
90|only for safety reasons.
91|At the end of the lead-in period, patients who required uptitration
92|of their insulin glargine dose based on the TTT algorithm (fasting
93|blood glucose ≥5.6 mmol/L in the previous week) were randomized
94|(1:1) to receive dulaglutide 1.5 mg once weekly or placebo once
95|weekly, added to insulin glargine with metformin and/or acarbose, for
96|28 weeks (herein referred to as dulaglutide/glargine and placebo/glar-
97|gine). Randomization was implemented using a computer-generated
98|random sequence and an interactive web response system and was
99|stratified by HbA1c (<8.5%, ≥8.5%) and OAD use (metformin alone,
100|acarbose alone, metformin, and acarbose). Treatment allocation was
101|masked from the patients, investigators, and sponsor staff who were
102|involved in the treatment or clinical evaluation of patients. To pre-
103|serve blinding, the study drug was provided in identical cartons con-
104|taining unmarked injection devices.

24|Outcomes
25|The primary study objective was to demonstrate the superiority of dula-
26|glutide 1.5 mg once weekly versus placebo when added to titrated insulin
27|glargine with metformin and/or acarbose, as assessed by change in HbA1c
28|from baseline to Week 28. Key secondary endpoints were the proportion
29|of patients achieving HbA1c <7.0% at Week 28 and change in body
30|weight and fasting serum glucose (FSG) from baseline to Week 28. Addi-
31|tional secondary endpoints were HbA1c ≤6.5% at Week 28, change in
32|blood glucose profile (based on SMBG) from baseline to Week 28, change
33|in daily mean insulin glargine dose from baseline, and the following com-
34|posite endpoints: the proportion of patients achieving HbA1c <7.0% with
35|no weight gain (<0.1 kg) at Week 28; the proportion of patients achieving
36|HbA1c <7.0% with no weight gain (<0.1 kg) at Week 28 and without
37|documented symptomatic hypoglycaemia (blood glucose <3.0 mmol/L)
38|during the maintenance period (Weeks 12-28); and the proportion of
39|patients achieving HbA1c <7.0% at Week 28 without documented symp-
40|tomatic hypoglycaemia (blood glucose <3.0 mmol/L) during the mainte-
41|nance period (Weeks 12-28). Other composite endpoints included the
42|proportion of patients achieving HbA1c <7.0% at Week 28 without docu-
43|mented symptomatic hypoglycaemia (blood glucose <3.9 mmol/L) during
44|the maintenance period (Weeks 12-28), and the same composite endpoint
45|including no weight gain (<0.1 kg) at Week 28.
46|All adverse events (AEs) were coded using the Medical Dictionary
47|for Regulatory Activities version 24.1. The relationship between AEs
48|and study treatment was based on the investigator's opinion. AEs of
49|special interest included hypoglycaemia, initiation of rescue therapy
50|due to severe, persistent hyperglycaemia, pancreatitis (adjudicated by
51|an independent committee of experts), thyroid neoplasms, deaths and
52|nonfatal cardiovascular events and allergic/hypersensitivity reactions.
53|Patients who developed severe, persistent hyperglycaemia based on
54|prespecified thresholds during the treatment period received a new

56|with study drug. Rescue therapy could be any locally approved

82|Statistical analysis
83|Approximately, 246 patients were required to demonstrate a treat-
84|ment difference of 0.5% for reduction in HbA1c from baseline to
85|Week 28 for dulaglutide versus placebo with 90% power, assuming a
86|standard deviation (SD) of 1.2% and at a two-sided alpha of 0.05.
87|Assuming a drop-out rate of 15%, target enrolment was approxi-
88|mately 290 randomized patients.
89|Efficacy and safety were analysed in all randomized patients who
90|received ≥1 dose of study drug for the assigned treatment arm (inten-
91|tion-to-treat population). For the efficacy analyses, data were cen-
92|sored from the point of initiating rescue therapy or on premature
93|discontinuation of study drug. The primary and key secondary contin-
94|uous efficacy measures were analysed using a mixed-model repeated
95|measures approach with a restricted maximum likelihood, with treat-
96|ment, OAD (metformin alone, acarbose alone, metformin and acar-
97|bose), visit and treatment-by-visit as fixed effects, baseline as a
98|covariate, and patient as a random effect. For the primary and key
99|secondary efficacy endpoints, a graphical testing scheme was used to
100|compare treatments, to control against a type I error.
101|Composite outcomes for the additional secondary endpoints
102|were analysed using a logistic regression model including treatment,
103|OAD and baseline HbA1c as factors, and using the last observation
104|carried forward to impute missing post-baseline data. For the safety
105|analysis, the number of hypoglycaemia event episodes was analysed
106|using a generalized mixed-effects model assuming a negative binomial
107|distribution, and including treatment, OAD, and baseline HbA1c stra-
108|tum (<8.5% vs. ≥8.5%). The logarithm of days between visits was
109|included to offset possible unequal duration between visits and
110|among patients. All other safety data were summarized descriptively.
111|All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4 or
112|higher.

46|Safety
47|Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were reported in 122 patients
48|(84.7%) who received dulaglutide/glargine and 109 patients (74.1%)
49|who received placebo/glargine. Patients in the dulaglutide/glargine
50|group had a higher incidence of decreased appetite and gastrointes-
51|tinal TEAEs than those in the placebo/glargine group (Table 2),
52|although all events were mild or moderate in intensity. Serious AEs
53|were reported in eight patients (5.6%) in the dulaglutide/glargine
54|group and 12 (8.2%) in the placebo/glargine group (Table 2). Four
55|patients in the dulaglutide/glargine group discontinued study drug
56|due to an AE (asthenia, decreased appetite, gastritis and gastrointes-
57|tinal disorders in one patient each). Four patients in the dulaglutide/
58|glargine group discontinued the study due to AEs; one patient had
59|acute kidney injury (discontinued study drug for unknown reason
60|[s] and discontinued the study 131 days later; judged not related to

34|DISCUSSION
35|The results of AWARD-CHN3 provide the first evidence supporting
36|the combination of a weekly GLP-1RA administered with basal insulin
37|in Chinese patients with T2DM. Consistent with previous findings in a
38|predominantly White population,17 our results show that adding
39|once-weekly dulaglutide to basal insulin improved glycaemic control,
40|reduced body weight and reduced basal insulin dose requirements,
41|without increasing the risk of hypoglycaemia, compared with placebo.
42|The safety of dulaglutide was consistent with its known safety profile
43|and with that of the GLP-1RA class in general.18,19 Accordingly, mild-
44|to-moderate gastrointestinal TEAEs were reported more frequently
45|with dulaglutide than with placebo. No cases of acute pancreatitis or
46|thyroid neoplasms were reported, and the incidence of hypersensitiv-
47|ity was low. Together with the AWARD-CHN1 and AWARD-CHN2
48|studies, these data indicate that once-weekly dulaglutide is an effica-
49|cious and safe therapeutic option for Chinese patients with diverse
50|treatment backgrounds, ranging from monotherapy to combined
51|treatment with OADs and basal insulin.20,21
52|In this study, dulaglutide added to basal insulin led to a signifi-
53|cantly greater reduction in HbA1c from baseline to Week 28 ( 1.0%;
54|95% CI -1.1 to 0.8) compared with placebo and basal insulin, as well
55|as significantly greater reductions in both FSG and postprandial blood
56|glucose. HbA1c targets were met by a higher proportion of patients
57|receiving dulaglutide versus placebo, 75.9% of whom achieved HbA1c
58|levels <7.0%, which is particularly high considering that the mean
59|duration of diabetes was 11.2 years. In contrast, in the predominantly
60|Asian population with T2DM in the GetGoal-L-C study, once-daily
61|administration of the GLP-1RA lixisenatide with basal insulin resulted
62|in a smaller difference in HbA1c reduction compared with placebo
63|plus basal insulin ( 0.5%; 95% CI -0.7 to 0.3).22 In addition, 37.3%
64|of patients who received lixisenatide/basal insulin in GetGoal-L-C
65|achieved HbA1c levels <7.0%.22 It should be noted that no direct
66|comparison of these trials can be made due to their different designs
67|and populations.
68|In addition to improving glycaemic control, dulaglutide may offer
69|advantages over other approved once-daily GLP-1RAs when added to
70|basal insulin in terms of dosing frequency and method of administra-
71|tion. In a preference study, adults with T2DM reported the ease of
72|use of the prefilled, single-use pen with which dulaglutide is adminis-
73|tered, favouring its needle characteristics (pre-attached, not visible,
74|and no needle handling required).23 This user-friendly injection experi-
75|ence is invaluable from a clinical perspective.
76|In the present study, dulaglutide added to insulin glargine signifi-
77|cantly reduced mean body weight, with a significant difference versus
78|placebo (treatment difference: 1.2 kg; P < 0.001), and without
79|increasing the incidence of hypoglycaemia. Furthermore, a higher pro-
80|portion of patients receiving dulaglutide achieved the composite out-
81|comes of HbA1c <7.0% without body weight gain and/or without
82|hypoglycaemia versus the placebo group. These data will aid clinical
83|decision making, as the avoidance of weight gain and hypoglycaemia
84|often deter patients and healthcare providers from adequate titration
85|of basal insulin.16 In the present study, after the 4-week insulin dose
86|stabilization period, the doses of insulin required in the placebo group
87|were significantly higher than those required in the dulaglutide
88|group at each timepoint until the end of the treatment period.
89|This trial has several strengths and limitations. A key strength is
90|the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design. In addition,
91|the titration of basal insulin using the TTT algorithm allowed the
92|individualization of treatment to be standardized and better reflects
93|contemporary clinical practice. Although the titration of basal insulin
94|could be a potential confounding factor compared with a fixed
95|insulin dosing strategy, compliance with the TTT insulin dose assess-
96|ment outcome was similar in both treatment groups. Another poten-
97|tial weakness was the choice of comparator, as intensive basal
98|insulin with metformin and/or acarbose is only one of the recom-
99|mended therapeutic options for the study population and the results
100|cannot be extrapolated or compared to other treatment regimens.
101|Finally, the study had a relatively short treatment period of
102|28 weeks; however, this is the same duration as that of the global
103|AWARD-9 study.17
104|Collectively, the results of AWARD-CHN3 are relevant for
105|Chinese patients with T2DM who are receiving basal insulin and one or
106|two OADs and are unable to achieve their glycaemic targets. Adding
107|once-weekly dulaglutide 1.5 mg to titrated basal insulin is an effective
108|and well-tolerated therapeutic option, with an improved risk-benefit
109|profile of greater glycaemic control and body weight reduction without
110|hypoglycaemia and a smaller increase in daily insulin dose.
