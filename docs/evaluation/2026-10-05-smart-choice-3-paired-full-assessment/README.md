## Anonymous review and its limits

A new AI reviewer (`gpt-6.1-sol`, low reasoning), not a human reviewer, received only anonymous final active answers, common Result, frozen numbered Source text and original packaged guidance, with no identities, telemetry, previous findings, gold labels or algorithmic judgments. The 0–3 rubric and comparison dimensions were frozen before outcome inspection. The reviewer scored 31 active answers and 50 Domain dimensions, then wrote immutable scores SHA-256 `4b65d1efbfc4f11af2ca1b5de65316c28bff4882a24c644bdf49d68c0729fdde`. Mapping was withheld from the reviewer through lock. Inputs remained unchanged and the reviewer reports no identity inference or external consultation.

**Procedural departure:** the operator accidentally opened the private mapping before the score lock. The reviewer remained isolated; no mapping, telemetry or scientific hints were sent and the scores were not revised. This prevents claiming complete operator blinding. Length/style differences also make anonymous presentation imperfect. The reviewer did not see official runtime question cards, activation receipts, revision history or actual-delivery traces, so this review does not establish algorithmic correctness. It used frozen text, not original PDF layout or participant-level data.

**Citation and scope:** all copied quotes literally matched their coordinates. Luna D3 nevertheless bound the true one-year completion claim to baseline risk factors (S2 p5 lines117–145); actual completion is p5 lines24–32. Literal quotation validation did not establish entailment. Sol used actual flow/completion evidence and separated randomized/analyzed counts, one-year completion and composite observation through the HR window.

**D3 materiality:** Sol weighed 62 early incomplete participants against 220 reported primary events (S2 p4 lines102–145; p6 lines6–23), rather than using a completion percentage alone. Neither count yields a corrected HR or proves actual bias. Sol still inferred probably no likely outcome-dependent loss from similar early loss, duration and vital-status linkage. The reviewer scored that answer 2: balanced loss does not establish noninformative censoring, particularly for nonfatal components; individual timing/reasons remain unknown. Death linkage is not complete composite observation, and ITT membership is not observed data.

**D5 chronology and correspondence:** Sol read and retained SAP censoring (S1 p98 lines4–14), the reported three-year cap (S2 p4 lines86–89), and Table 2 overall-period wording (S2 p6 lines149–156). Luna preserved generic access caveats but did not confront this specific discrepancy. A dated SAP and matching endpoint/model do not establish finalization before all investigator unblinded access. Pooled blinded recruitment analysis (S2 p4 lines50–64) offers scoped reassurance, not that chronology proof. Sol's NI retained an unresolved correspondence issue; it does not establish result-driven selection or a preferred adverse label. The independent reviewer also noted inconsistent recruitment-extension prespecification wording (S2 p10 lines69–74), without resolving it by assertion.

**D2 defensible disagreement:** Luna NI on context-caused deviations and Sol probably no were both judged defensible. Sol additionally used actual medication use and ordinary discretionary care (S1 p27 lines3–30; p28 lines11–76; S2 p10 lines127–132), while retaining unknown individual reasons and prescribing effects (p10 lines56–68). Different answer polarity alone did not earn scores.

**D1/D4:** both had strong conducted randomization, masking, ITT and blinded-adjudication warrants. Sol more clearly attributed independent allocation operation as planned, not confirmed implementation, and bounded unmeasured unscheduled testing. No clinical bias effect is inferred from those differences.

Active paths differed (14 Luna answers; 17 Sol). Their answer-score sums (31/42 and 48/51) are recorded in the locked review but are not comparable aggregate performance measures. No inactive branch was scored zero.
