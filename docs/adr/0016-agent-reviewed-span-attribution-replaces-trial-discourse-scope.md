# Agent-reviewed span attribution replaces Trial discourse scope

Issue #151 showed that unit-level parser labels such as `active`, `same_trial`, and `other_trial` are too brittle to determine which Trial a passage concerns: ordinary unlabelled PDF text becomes permanently ineligible even after an attributable review resolves its meaning. We remove Trial discourse scope from parser output, canonical units, retrieval, warnings, and Evidence eligibility, refining ADR-0002's separation of visibility from eligibility. Retrieval exposes source-preserving candidates, while each exact reviewed span records its own `active`, `other`, `not_explicit`, or `unresolved` Trial attribution from bounded reading context; only `active` supporting or contradicting spans qualify as Evidence, and unresolved retained spans block freeze. Candidate reviews are single-question (`sq_id`) and may aggregate multiple independently attributed spans, but no Source-wide or candidate-wide Trial label authorizes citation. `read_evidence` issues an opaque, persistent read-view receipt; the engine resolves that receipt into an immutable reviewed-context descriptor with exact snapshot, continuation, displayed fragments, bounds, hashes, and lineage. Navigation handles remain ephemeral reading inputs. The engine derives stable span identities from candidate and canonical lineage plus exact bounds, and makes each textual Evidence claim depend directly on its authorizing review revision and span. Question-specific bundles contain only their own reviews. This is a breaking Evidence-contract revision with no compatibility aliases or ledger reinterpretation: invalid review provenance fails closed as unresolvable Evidence, while correctly linked superseded dependencies are stale Evidence. The decision deliberately trades automatic Trial filtering for broader inspection and attributable agent judgment while retaining deterministic provenance, completeness, and terminal validation.
