38|BACKGROUND
39|The timing and indications for surgical intervention in asymptomatic patients with
40|severe aortic stenosis remain controversial.
41|METHODS
42|In a multicenter trial, we randomly assigned 145 asymptomatic patients with very
43|severe aortic stenosis (defined as an aortic-valve area of ≤0.75 cm2 with either an
44|aortic jet velocity of ≥4.5 m per second or a mean transaortic gradient of ≥50 mm Hg)
45|to early surgery or to conservative care according to the recommendations of cur-
46|rent guidelines. The primary end point was a composite of death during or within
47|30 days after surgery (often called operative mortality) or death from cardiovascular
48|causes during the entire follow-up period. The major secondary end point was death
49|from any cause during follow-up.
50|RESULTS
51|In the early-surgery group, 69 of 73 patients (95%) underwent surgery within 2 months
52|after randomization, and there was no operative mortality. In an intention-to-treat
53|analysis, a primary end-point event occurred in 1 patient in the early-surgery group
54|(1%) and in 11 of 72 patients in the conservative-care group (15%) (hazard ratio,
55|0.09; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.01 to 0.67; P = 0.003). Death from any cause
56|occurred in 5 patients in the early-surgery group (7%) and in 15 patients in the con-
57|servative-care group (21%) (hazard ratio, 0.33; 95% CI, 0.12 to 0.90). In the con-
58|servative-care group, the cumulative incidence of sudden death was 4% at 4 years
59|and 14% at 8 years.
60|CONCLUSIONS
61|Among asymptomatic patients with very severe aortic stenosis, the incidence of the
62|composite of operative mortality or death from cardiovascular causes during the
63|follow-up period was significantly lower among those who underwent early aortic-
64|valve replacement surgery than among those who received conservative care. (Fund-
65|ed by the Korean Institute of Medicine; RECOVERY ClinicalTrials.gov number,
66|NCT01161732.)

36|Aortic Stenosis (RECOVERY) trial was designed

47|Methods
48|Trial Design and Oversight
49|We conducted this multicenter, randomized, par-
50|allel-group, open-label trial involving asymptom-
51|atic patients with very severe aortic stenosis who
52|were candidates for either early surgery or conser-
53|vative care at four medical centers in Korea. The
54|trial protocol (available with the full text of this
55|article at NEJM.org) was designed by the principal
56|investigator and approved by the institutional re-
57|view board at each participating center. The Ko-
58|rean Institute of Medicine, which supported the
59|trial coordinators during the first 2 years of the
60|trial, had no role in the collection, analysis, or
61|interpretation of the data, writing of the manu-
62|script, or any other aspect of the trial. The trial
63|received no other external sources of funding.
64|The trial was conducted in accordance with
65|the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.
66|An independent clinical-events committee ad-
67|judicated all serious adverse events, and a data
68|and safety monitoring board oversaw the safety
69|of the trial. The first draft of the manuscript
70|was prepared by the first author and was re-
71|viewed and edited by all the authors. All the
72|authors made the decision to submit the manu-
73|script for publication and vouch for the accu-
74|racy and completeness of the data and for the
75|fidelity of the trial to the protocol.
76|Patient Selection
77|We screened consecutive patients who were 20 to
78|80 years of age and who presented with very se-
79|vere aortic stenosis, which was assessed by means
80|of transthoracic echocardiography. In accordance
81|with the 1998 American College of Cardiology-
82|American Heart Association (ACC-AHA) guide-
83|lines and a traditional definition of severe aortic
84|stenosis,10,11 we defined very severe aortic steno-
85|sis as an aortic-valve area of 0.75 cm2 or less
86|with either a peak aortic jet velocity of 4.5 m per
87|second or greater or a mean transaortic gradient
88|of 50 mm Hg or greater.
89|In accordance with the 2006 ACC-AHA guide-
90|lines on surgical indications for severe aortic
91|stenosis,12 patients were excluded if they had ex-
92|ertional dyspnea, syncope, presyncope or angina,
93|a left ventricular ejection fraction of less than 50%,
94|or clinically significant aortic regurgitation or
95|mitral valve disease or if they had undergone
96|cardiac surgery. Exercise testing was selectively
97|performed to evaluate patients with nonspecific
98|symptoms, and patients with a positive exercise
99|test were excluded. We also excluded patients who
100|were not candidates for early surgery because of
101|age (>80 years) or a medical condition such as
102|cancer. All the participants provided written in-
103|formed consent.

4|Trial Procedures
5|Eligibility was determined after each patient un-
6|derwent a thorough evaluation of symptoms and
7|medical records and results of echocardiography
8|and exercise testing were reviewed. Details re-
9|garding echocardiography and exercise testing are
10|provided in the Supplementary Appendix, available
11|at NEJM.org. Patients were randomly assigned in
12|a 1:1 ratio to early surgery or conservative care
13|with the use of a Web-based interactive response
14|system. The assignment to each treatment group
15|was computer-generated and stratified according
16|to the participating center by means of a permuted-
17|block sequence with variable block size.
18|The protocol specified that patients assigned
19|to the early-surgery group should undergo aortic-
20|valve replacement within 2 months after random-
21|ization. Patients assigned to the conservative-care
22|group received treatment according to the ACC-
23|AHA guidelines,2,12 and they were referred for
24|surgery if they became symptomatic during fol-
25|low-up, if the left ventricular ejection fraction
26|decreased to less than 50%, or if the peak aortic
27|jet velocity increased each year by more than 0.5 m
28|per second on follow-up echocardiography. Details
29|regarding surgical procedures and patient follow-
30|up are provided in the Supplementary Appendix.
31|Trial End Points
32|The primary end point was a composite of op-
33|erative mortality or death from cardiovascular
34|causes during the follow-up period (continuing
35|until 4 years after the last patient was enrolled).
36|Prespecified secondary end points included death
37|from any cause, repeat aortic-valve surgery, clini-
38|cal thromboembolic events, and hospitalization
39|for heart failure during follow-up. Specific defi-
40|nitions of trial end points are provided in the
41|Supplementary Appendix.
42|Statistical Analysis
43|On the basis of our previous observational study,13
44|we estimated that a sample of 144 patients would
45|provide the trial with 80% power, at a two-sided
46|significance level of 0.05, to detect a significant
47|difference with respect to the primary end point,
48|assuming that the incidence of the primary end
49|point would be 16% in the conservative-care group
50|and 2% in the early-surgery group during a follow-
51|up period that continued until 4 years after the last
52|patient was enrolled. In calculating the sample size
53|by a log-rank survival power analysis, we also
54|assumed that an enrollment period of 2 years
55|would be needed to complete enrollment and that
56|the rate of loss to follow-up would be 10%.
57|Analyses were performed on an intention-to-
58|treat basis. Differences between the treatment
59|groups were evaluated with the use of Student's
60|t-test for continuous variables and Fisher's exact
61|test for categorical variables. Because random-
62|ization was stratified according to the partici-
63|pating center, we analyzed the end points with
64|the use of stratified Cox proportional-hazards re-
65|gression with Firth correction. Estimates of cu-
66|mulative incidences were calculated by the Kaplan-
67|Meier method and were compared with the use
68|of the log-rank test. For the Kaplan-Meier analy-
69|sis, we analyzed all clinical events according to
70|the time to the first event. Hazard ratios with
71|95% confidence intervals were derived with the
72|use of the stratified Cox proportional-hazards
73|model with Firth correction. The 95% confidence
74|intervals have not been adjusted for multiple com-
75|parisons, and therefore inferences drawn from
76|these intervals may not be reproducible.
77|For the primary end-point analysis, we also
78|performed a competing-risk analysis in which
79|death due to a cause other than a cardiovascular
80|cause was considered as a competing risk, and
81|hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals were
82|calculated with the use of the method of Fine and
83|Gray. The cumulative incidences of the primary
84|end point were also compared between the treat-
85|ment groups with the use of Gray's test. Subgroup
86|analyses were performed to determine whether
87|the result of the primary end point was consistent
88|in two prespecified subgroups defined according
89|to peak aortic velocity and cause of aortic stenosis.
90|A per-protocol analysis of the primary end point
91|was also performed. All reported P values were
92|two-sided, and a P value of less than 0.05 was
93|considered to indicate statistical significance. We
94|used SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute), for
95|statistical analyses.

27|Discussion
28|The RECOVERY trial compared early surgery with
29|conservative care in asymptomatic patients with
30|very severe aortic stenosis. The trial showed a
31|lower incidence of the primary end point of opera-
32|tive mortality or death from cardiovascular causes
33|during the follow-up period among patients who
34|underwent early surgery than among those who
35|received conservative care. Moreover, early surgery
36|was associated with a lower incidence of death
37|from any cause among such patients.
38|The decision to perform surgery in an asymp-
39|tomatic patient requires careful weighing of the
40|risks of early aortic-valve replacement against those
41|of observation. In patients with asymptomatic se-
42|vere aortic stenosis, it has generally appeared to
43|be relatively safe to follow a watchful waiting
44|strategy and delay surgery until symptoms de-
45|velop.14 However, this conservative care strategy
46|is also associated with a risk of sudden death,
47|denial or late reporting of symptoms by patients,
48|irreversible myocardial damage, and an increase
49|in surgical risk while waiting for symptoms to
50|develop.4-6 In previous observational studies, base-
51|line differences between the treatment groups,
52|treatment-selection bias, and unmeasured con-
53|founders might have influenced the results.13,15
54|By reducing these limitations inherent to observa-
55|tional studies,4,16 this randomized trial provides
56|evidence to support early aortic-valve replacement
57|in asymptomatic patients with very severe aortic
58|stenosis.

7| Statistical Analysis Plan

9|Sample Size Calculation
10|We tried to determine the rate of primary endpoint based on the randomized trials, but no randomized
11|trial has been conducted to evaluate outcomes of early surgery. Using a prospective registry data, we
12|recently reported that the estimated actuarial 6-year survival and cardiac mortality-free survival rates
13|were 98±1% and 100% in the early surgery group, 68±6% and 76±5% in the conventional treatment
14|group, respectively (p < 0.001) (9). Based on our prospective observational study, we estimated that a
15|sample size of 144 patients would provide 80% power to detect a significant difference with respect to
16|the primary end point at the 2-sided significance level of 0.05, assuming that the 4-year rate of a
17|composite end point (cardiac mortality or operative mortality) would be 16% in the conventional
18|treatment group and 2% in the early surgery group.
19|
20|Analysis of primary and secondary end point
21|All primary and secondary endpoints will be analyzed on an intent-to-treat basis (all patients analyzed
22|as part of their assigned treatment group). For intent-to-treat analysis, all patients who sign the written
23|informed consent form and are randomized in the study will be included in the analysis, regardless of
24|whether or not the correct treatment is administered, or whether crossover occurs.
25|To analyze outcomes, estimates of cumulative event rates will be calculated by the Kaplan-Meier
26|method and compared employing the log-rank test. For Kaplan-Meier analysis, we will analyze all
27|clinical events by time to first event. Hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals will be derived with
28|the use of the Cox proportional hazards model.
29|Subgroup analyses will be performed to determine whether the reduction in the primary end point after
30|early surgery is consistent in 2 prespecified subgroups of peak aortic velocity (<5 m/s vs. ≥5 m/s) and
31|etiology of AS (degenerative vs. non-degenerative), using Cox regression models with tests for
32|interaction.
