## Scientific review

3.1PN is a qualified, defensible conservative inference from incomplete visits, actual imputation reporting and unknown endpoint-specific availability. It is not established merely by missing exact counts; a contextual near-all response remains possible. Treatment completion involves adherence and attending visits, so it is relevant indirect information while not identical to urine ascertainment. Neither the study-wide77/82 retention figure nor all-analyzed denominators establishes observation in this47participant comparison.

3.2PN avoids treating MAR/Bayesian imputation or age/sex adjustment as automatic proof of no bias. But the explanation under-discusses explicitly reported baselineweek0 adjustment, timefixed effects, participant random intercept and gamma outcome model. Those could inform the plausibility of conditional MAR; they should be evaluated rather than demanding a named MNAR sensitivity analysis. The model's source selection stops author7atline24, before the age/sex detail on25–27, although it read through44. Thus that particular claim is available in delivered text but incompletely anchored by its attached selection. This is a citation/warrant limitation, not a rule that3.2 must beYes orNI.

A factual error is retained: it also cites **poor adherence**, which Figure1 assigns to the eliminated **200mg** arm, outside the approved comparison. Its counterevidence repeats that broader reason pool. The correct selected-arm reasons are loss to follow-up and missed scheduled visits in placebo, and psychotropic medication use in CBD400. The possible-dependence warrant can remain without the imported reason; the operator did not repair or relabel the assessment.

The probable response appropriately retains uncertainty. Neither exact observed counts, mandatory full supplementary reading, nor a formal worst-case bound is required to permit judgment.
