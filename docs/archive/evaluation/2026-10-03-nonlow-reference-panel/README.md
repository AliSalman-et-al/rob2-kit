## Sources and contestability

**Attal.** The newly recovered [author-deposited review](https://hal.science/hal-05059767/document) identifies responder outcomes for the synthesis and says RoB was assessed for the primary outcome (physical p3); the referent cannot be safely resolved against the metadata without the published appendix. Its official [journal record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1474442225000687) and supplement asset returned 403 in this environment. No challenge or paywall was bypassed. The original Code trial report, physical pp4–6, distinguishes continuous pain from 50% response at week24: 10/34 versus5/32 for response, versus an adjusted continuous effect −0.77. The report describes LOCF, 68 randomized, 66 analyzed and 52 completing the study. These facts make D3 substantive: imputation does not establish availability or eliminate missingness bias; the actual missing-data mechanism, result susceptibility and sensitivity evidence would determine whether High is defensible. D5 Some concerns requires analysis/measurement eligibility and selection rationale, rather than multiplicity alone. No human signalling rationale was recovered. Neither non-Low rating is independently verified beyond the archived metadata in this pass. Selecting the week24 responder result could be justified only by verified reference binding; it is not silently substituted for the original continuous request.

**Gupta.** The official [review](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2834481) and [publisher supplement](https://cdn.jamanetwork.com/ama/content_public/journal/peds/939668/poi250021supp1_prod_1753824082.25358.pdf) expose the primary trial contrast/time (eTable3, physical p5), and the rating grid (p12). The grid matches all archived domain labels, including overall Low despite D2 Some concerns. Preserve that published inconsistency; do not recalculate a human overall or change labels. The main review gives a group-level concern about treatment in expectant-management arms, but not Gupta's question-by-question rationale. Its outcomes include mortality at36weeks versus discharge, BPD composites and gestational-age subgroups. The same-trial primary in eTable3 supplies the reconstruction anchor; the report has one main primary result and supportive subgroup/secondary analyses. Explicitly assume the RoB row applies to that primary, without asserting author confirmation or transfer to mortality-only/discharge/subgroup outcomes. Those review outcomes remain distinct rather than being treated as co-primary trial results.

The original trial report, physical pp3,6,9, describes prespecified rescue-treatment criteria, ITT with missing-only exclusions, 220/318 versus202/318 for the main composite, and a sensitivity analysis excluding noncriteria open-label treatment. Whether rescue was allowed under the protocol, arose from trial context, affected outcomes and was balanced makes the human D2 concern scientifically contestable; rescue itself does not settle those questions. The primary 36-week window is not replaced by the review's discharge-based variants. The original appendix TableS3 (physical14) specifies rescue criteria. TableS10 (physical22) records noncriteria open-label treatment in15/324 versus33/322, and TableS9 on the same page gives the restricted sensitivity primary RR1.09 (0.98–1.22). This is more specific than ordinary allowed rescue: potential trial-context deviation and imbalance need appraisal, while the sensitivity analysis does not by itself prove no bias. These original passages and the publisher reference sources complete the declared matching/D2 preparation evidence; they are not a new model assessment. Detailed human signalling responses are absent.

**Castoldi.** The official [review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/03635465241304781) Figure2 directly verifies D1 High and D2–D5 Low. Table1 identifies 61/60 knees and228months; Table3 includes self-reported function and clinical outcomes, and the narrative discusses osteoarthritis. Although the review says RoB2 assesses a single result, it does not identify which of these its Castoldi row uses. The original Code report, physical pp2–3, describes unblinded block randomization, an independent follow-up examiner, primary subjective IKDC and secondary Lysholm, graft failure and OA outcomes. D1 High needs evidence concerning sequence/concealment or imbalances; unblinded surgery alone does not establish inadequate allocation concealment before enrollment. Outcome-specific missingness and assessment also make wholesale transfer of Low D3/D4 questionable. These are prospective adjudication questions, not assertions that the reviewers were wrong. The public figure supplies ratings, not signalling explanations.
