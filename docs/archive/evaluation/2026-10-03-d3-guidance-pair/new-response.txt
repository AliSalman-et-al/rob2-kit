**Case A — probably yes.** Outcome-specific follow-up was approximately 95% of expected in both arms, supporting an inference of near-complete follow-up for the selected time-to-first-event result (A3–A4). This is follow-up person-time, not a participant-level observation fraction, and 99% known vital status does not establish ascertainment of nonfatal events (A4). The evidence suggests limited missingness, but its potential impact cannot be pinned down without individual loss timing, exact observation counts, or sensitivity results (A5–A6). Those unknowns temper the judgment; they do not establish materially incomplete availability.

**Case B — no_information.** Including all randomized patients in the Cox analysis and specifying last endpoint contact as the censoring rule do not establish how many had endpoint data available (B2, B4). Known vital status for 99% does not establish ascertainment of the nonfatal components, and actual composite follow-up, ascertainment, and losses are unreported (B4–B6). Thus neither the extent of missing outcome data nor whether it could importantly affect the estimate can be reasonably inferred from the supplied evidence.
