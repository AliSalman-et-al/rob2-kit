**2.6 — Appropriate analysis: probably no.** The article says the ITT population included all 291 randomized participants, but also says efficacy data were censored from the point of rescue therapy or premature study drug discontinuation (main article, p. 3, lines 89–93). Figure 2 confirms that the reported analyses excluded data after either event (p. 5, lines 6–8). This is therefore not a complete analysis of the assignment effect through Week 28: it removes post-randomization follow-up based on events that may relate to outcome. Rescue therapy affected only one participant, in the dulaglutide group (p. 7, lines 7–9), which limits that part of the concern. The article does not report how many participants discontinued study drug, had post-discontinuation HbA1c measurements, or were consequently omitted from this specific MMRM result. The analysis may still use observed pre-censoring HbA1c measurements, so the evidence supports a probable judgment rather than certainty that eligible participants with observed outcomes were inappropriately excluded.

**2.7 — Conditional impact: probably no.** This follows the probable concern in 2.6. The only reported rescue-therapy case was one participant; the trial randomized 144 and 147 participants per arm (main article, pp. 3, lines 119–120; p. 7, lines 7–9). The larger uncertainty is drug discontinuation: the article reports discontinuation events, but does not establish how many affected the Week 28 HbA1c analysis or whether post-discontinuation HbA1c values were available. For example, four dulaglutide participants discontinued study drug due to adverse events, and one participant discontinued after acute kidney injury, but the article does not connect these cases to HbA1c measurement availability or the specific analysis (p. 6, lines 55–62). This leaves some potential influence unresolved; the documented scale and the strong Week 28 difference do not suggest a substantial impact is probable. No precise impact estimate is available.
