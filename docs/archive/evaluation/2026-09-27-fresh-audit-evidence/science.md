# Scientific disagreement audit: 2026-09-27 fresh campaign

Scope: the selected results in `eval/runs/2026-09-27/rob2-trial-benchmark-fresh-4e03e798` (campaign `9da4171216eb4d06a40889994e584a0f`). The trace ledger contains 47 domain-label disagreements (12 D2, 14 D3, 7 D4, 14 D5). I reviewed the saved rationales, then source-checked the strongest D2 candidates and selected D3–D5 cases below. This is calibrated source triage, not expert re-adjudication of every result; the separate exact-Result/scope audit is not repeated.

Cochrane RoB 2 judgments apply to a specified result. For effect of assignment, D2 concerns deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the trial context and affected the outcome; open-label status alone is not sufficient. D3 depends on actual outcome availability and whether missingness relates to the true value, with no universal percentage threshold. D4 includes differential measurement or unequal opportunities to detect events. D5 concerns result-dependent selection among eligible estimates and asks whether the analysis plan was finalized before unblinded data were available. The official tool allows “No information” only when a probable answer is unreasonable in the trial circumstances; use the resulting algorithm category rather than adding a separate post-algorithm severity rule ([Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 8 §§8.2.2–8.2.3, 8.4.2, 8.5–8.7](https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-08)).

## Source-checked findings

### PEACE-1 adverse events, D4: model High vs reference Some Concerns

The model’s `differential = yes` has a direct source basis. The supplement gives abiraterone arms B/D additional AST, ALT, and bilirubin checks every two weeks for three months, at month 5, and monthly thereafter (supplement PDF p.14, printed p.13). The approved grade ≥3 adverse-event result includes laboratory toxicities; the article grades events with CTCAE v4.0 and uses the highest grade during treatment (main article PDF p.4, printed p.1698). Cochrane identifies extra visits that create more opportunities to detect events as a D4 mechanism (§8.6). The schedule therefore supports the Yes branch for this aggregate result; the model’s High follows the domain algorithm. Do not require a separate quantified material-effect threshold after that accepted answer. Confidence: **high** that the differential-ascertainment warrant is real; the reference label remains a human-label deviation, not proof the model reasoned incorrectly.

### TITAN overall survival, D3: model High vs reference Low

The report says 45 participants who stopped treatment remained in survival follow-up, while 39 were lost to follow-up or withdrew from all further data collection (main article PDF p.4, printed p.16). This is concrete missingness evidence, but the reported count does not establish that loss depended on true survival or resolve the model’s `likely-dependent = no information` answer. Disease severity and withdrawal make dependence conceivable, not demonstrated. I cannot select a different signaling answer from this packet; follow the official answer path and do not recast the model’s High as verified or erroneous. Confidence: **low** on which domain label is best supported.

### ARASENS overall survival, D3: model High vs reference Low

The 1305/1306 full-analysis-set count does not establish near-complete survival follow-up. Censoring at last known alive is a standard rule, not an accounting of how many randomized participants had outcome data at each censoring time. The article reports 533 deaths and median follow-up of 43.7 versus 42.4 months (main article PDF p.4, printed p.1135); the flow material also lists an unquantified lost-to-follow-up category. Arm-specific loss counts, censoring reasons, and their relation to true survival are unavailable. Availability and dependence therefore remain unresolved, and I cannot prefer the provisional Low or model High from these facts. Confidence: **low**.

### TITAN progression-free survival, D5: model Low vs reference Some Concerns

Protocol Amendment 4 dated 5 September 2018 specifies one rPFS analysis, ITT, and the primary time-to-event methods (protocol PDF pp.123, 150; printed amendment p.122); the reported primary result matches those methods (main article PDF pp.3–5, printed pp.15–17). This shows a dated plan before the November 23, 2018 analysis cutoff. It does **not** establish that the plan predated access to unblinded outcome data. The later SAP date also does not establish result-based selection. Since D5’s timing premise remains unresolved, neither the model Low nor reference Some Concerns is confirmed by the dated amendment alone. Confidence: **low-to-medium**.

### STAMPEDE progression-free survival, D5: model Some Concerns vs reference Low

SAP v2 is dated 31 January 2017 and covers PFS; the article says data were frozen 10 February 2017 (main article PDF p.3, printed p.340). The same page reports an open-label trial, independent DMC review of accruing data, and access to raw or committee-released data by some authors, but it does not date unblinded access relative to SAP finalization. The captured sources do not show that PFS was selected because of its result. This remains a timing/probability judgment under §8.7, not evidence that either label is wrong. Confidence: **low-to-medium**.

## D2 rationale scan

Three saved rationales expose the key distinction between “not documented” and “probably absent,” but none establishes a definite scientific error.

- **PEACE-1 overall survival (model Low; reference Some Concerns):** the article explicitly reports no masking, but says post-progression treatment followed common practice and investigators could change therapy as appropriate (main article PDF p.3); it also documents widespread later salvage treatment (PDF p.11). The efficacy comparison used ITT. Under §8.4.2, awareness alone does not establish trial-context-caused deviations. The model Low is defensible; the reference Some Concerns is not compelled by open-label conduct.
- **GETUG-AFU-15 PFS (model Some Concerns; reference Low):** the model’s No-information rationale mentions two docetaxel discontinuations classified as protocol violations, with no cause/timing evidence, and crossover to docetaxel after progression. The source places crossover at castration-resistant progression (main article PDF p.4, printed p.152), after a PFS progression event; that crossover cannot explain an earlier PFS result. This is an irrelevant sub-warrant for PFS. However, two actual protocol violations remain unexplained, so the source does not justify a confident “probably no” on all context-caused deviations. The overall D2 label is unresolved, not a confirmed model error.
- **TITAN adverse events (model Some Concerns; reference Low):** the saved rationale returns No information because adverse-event discontinuations and possible awareness are not linked to protocol-inconsistent deviations caused by trial context. The trial was double-blind/placebo-controlled and the report says patients and trial personnel remained unaware of randomization codes through the first interim analysis (main article PDF pp.1–3; supplement blinding description). Stopping for adverse events is expected in this setting, and no source documents unblinding-driven care changes. That makes No information look potentially over-conservative; however, possible unblinding or individual medical unblinding cannot be excluded from the packet. Treat as a candidate for calibration review, not a demonstrated error.

The open-label matching-label cases also matter: CHAARTED OS, ENZAMET OS, and STAMPEDE PFS record awareness but Low D2. This is scientifically coherent where sources do not identify context-caused deviations affecting the assignment-based result. Across D2, missing proof of absence should not automatically become “probably no,” but neither should open-label status, ordinary discontinuation, or post-progression treatment automatically become a D2 concern.

## Residual assessment

The scan found one clearly irrelevant sub-warrant (post-progression crossover used in the GETUG PFS rationale) and two plausible over-cautious No-information choices (TITAN AE D2 and, less strongly, GETUG PFS D2). In each, other source facts preserve genuine uncertainty. The irrelevant GETUG sub-warrant is a reasoning weakness, but the bounded source checks did not establish a uniquely wrong final Domain label with a source-justified replacement. The principal risk is calibration: avoid treating every unresolved premise as affirmative evidence, while retaining the official RoB 2 signaling question and algorithm. Do not add a new manual severity gate after the algorithm.
